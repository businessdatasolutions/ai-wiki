>

> - Pacesetters are 4x more likely to move AI pilots into production and 50% more likely to report measurable value.
> - 95% measure value (global: 32%).

> - Customer experience improving: 90% vs. 67%.
> - Innovation accelerating: 91% vs. 64%.
>

>
> **AI infrastructure debt:** 54% rank high compute costs as a challenge. 58% say scale and speed limit deployments. 29% are fully equipped to detect or prevent AI-specific threats. 26% have robust GPUs. 28% say outdated systems slow AI returns. 54% say their infrastructure can't scale for rising workloads.

> **AI proficiency:** A Cisco blurb says 75% of Pacesetters report staff AI proficiency, compared with 16% of others.

## 2. "AI Readiness Index Methodology"

Tool output, verbatim:

> **Survey vendor:** Not named. The page says an independent research partner ran the survey double-blind, so respondents didn't know Cisco commissioned it.
>
> **Sample size (2023, 2024, 2025):** Not provided. The page has a "senior leaders surveyed" label with no figure, and no year-specific counts appear.
>
> **Fieldwork dates:** Not provided.
>
> **Markets and industries:** The page labels "markets represented" and "industries included" but shows no counts.
>
> **Respondent criteria:** Senior business and technology leaders who are driving AI for their organizations and industries.
>
> **Indicators:** 49 indicators.
>
> **Pillars (six):** Strategy, Infrastructure, Data, Governance, Talent, and Culture.
>
> **Pillar weights:** Each pillar is weighted by its relative importance to overall AI readiness. The page doesn't give the specific weight values.
>
> **Overall score calculation:** 1. Each indicator is scored by deployment level: full deployment is 100%, partial deployment is 25–50%, and no deployment is 0%. 2. Indicator scores are combined into a score for each pillar and an overall score. The page doesn't state the exact aggregation formula.
>
> **Tier thresholds:** Applied at both the pillar level and the overall level.
> - Pacesetters: score ≥ 86 (Fully Prepared)
> - Chasers: score 61–85 (Moderately Prepared)
> - Followers: score 31–60 (Limited Preparedness)
> - Laggards: score 0–30 (Unprepared)

## 5. "How CEOs see AI in 2026" (CEO study, not an Index edition)

Tool output, verbatim:

> **Publication date:** Not stated on the page. Fieldwork was January 2026.
>
> **Key statistics (global):** 91% of CEOs are more optimistic about AI's potential than 12 months ago. 60% say AI beat their expectations in 2025. 65% fear their business is missing opportunities due to lack of investment. 53% say understanding of AI is no longer a barrier, up from 26% a year earlier. 72% expect humans to remain accountable by 2030. 7% expect almost no AI involvement by 2030. AI agent deployment is a top-three CEO priority for 2026.
>
> **Pacesetters (top 13% of organizations in Cisco's AI Readiness Index):** 77% plan to build new data centers in the next 12 months. 100% provide internal training, and 64% also use external experts (more than double the global average). 61% have mapped out what their agents will do, versus 32% globally. 95% have defined AI impact metrics, and 65% measure their training programs. Globally, only a third do both. 62% have fully integrated AI use cases into security and governance, versus 28% globally.
>
> **The 13% figure:** The page says Pacesetters are the top 13% of the most AI-ready organizations. It also says that globally, just 13% of organizations are at that level.
>
> **Methodology and sample:** Conducted by Opinion Matters. 2,511 CEOs (the introduction rounds this to 2,500) from companies with 250+ employees. Regions: Asia, Australia, Europe, North America, South America, and Africa. Data was collected in January 2026.

Note: several Pacesetter figures on this page (77% new data-center capacity, 95% impact metrics, 62% security integration) repeat figures from the October 2025 Index release, which suggests the "top 13%" here is the 2025 Index group, not a new measurement among CEOs. The page does not say so explicitly.
