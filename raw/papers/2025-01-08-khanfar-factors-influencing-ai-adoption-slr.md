Abstract
Purpose
Despite the potential of artificial intelligence (AI) systems to increase revenue, reduce costs,
and enhance performance, their adoption by organisations has fallen short of expectations,
leading to unsuccessful implementations. This paper aims to identify and elucidate the factors
influencing AI adoption at both the organisational and individual levels. Developing a
conceptual model, it contributes to understanding the underlying individual, social,
technological, organisational, and environmental factors and guides future research in this area.
Design/Methodology/Approach
The authors have conducted a systematic literature review to synthesise the literature on the
determinants of AI adoption. 90 papers published in the field of AI adoption in the
organisational context were reviewed to identify a set of factors influencing AI adoption.
Findings
This study categorised the factors influencing AI system adoption into individual, social,
organisational, environmental, and technological factors. Firm-level factors were found to
impact employee behaviour towards AI systems. Further research is needed to understand the
effects of these factors on employee perceptions, emotions, and behaviours towards new AI
systems. These findings led to the proposal of a theory-based model illustrating the
relationships between these factors, challenging the assumption of independence between
adoption influencers at both the firm and employee levels.
Originality
This study is one of the first to synthesise current knowledge on determinants of AI adoption,
serving as a theoretical foundation for further research in this emerging field. The adoption
model developed integrates key factors from both the firm and individual levels, offering a
holistic view of the interconnectedness of various AI adoption factors. This approach
challenges the assumption that factors at the firm and individual levels operate independently.
Through this study, information systems researchers and practitioners gain a deeper
understanding of AI adoption, enhancing their insight into its potential impacts.
Keywords: Artificial Intelligence (AI); Technology Adoption; Technology-OrganisationEnvironment (TOE); Digitalisation; Technology Acceptance Model (TAM)


1. Introduction
Artificial intelligence (AI) systems have transformed the business environment and changed
how organisations perform their operations and tasks (Tjebane et al., 2022). Organisations are
increasingly adopting AI systems because of the capabilities and opportunities they provide
(Oppioli et al., 2023). Many organisations have assigned resources and invested time and
money to successfully adopt AI systems. However, the adoption of AI by organisations remains
challenging, and the failure rate of AI projects is high (Makarius et al., 2020). Statistics from
2020 show that 70% of AI projects did not achieve their deemed benefits, leading many
organisations to drop their plans for AI projects. In addition, the failure rate of AI projects
between 2019 and 2020 increased, and AI project cancellation rates increased from 20% to
40% (Makarius et al., 2020). To combat this phenomenon and contribute to the success of AI
adoption projects, this research takes several foundational steps by; (1) providing structure to
the literature on the determinants of AI adoption at the firm and individual levels, (2) proposing
a comprehensive model that demonstrates the associations between different types of
determinants and how they shape the decision to adopt AI at the firm and individual levels, and
(3) identifying gaps in the literature and providing directions for future research. To
successfully adopt and implement AI systems and gain benefits from investment in AI, it is
crucial for managers to know the drivers of and barriers to AI adoption. From a practical
perspective, this study provides managers with a deep understanding of the determinants of AI
adoption that they should consider before embarking on such initiatives or investing in them.
Therefore, the determinants of AI adoption and implementation have gained increasing
attention in recent years (Tjebane et al., 2022; Na et al., 2022). Previous studies have
investigated the determinants of AI adoption at both the firm and employee levels. Although
previous studies have used various theories and models such as the ‘Technology-OrganisationEnvironment” (TOE) model, “Diffusion of Innovations’ (DOI), ‘Unified Theory of Acceptance
and Use of Technology’ (UTAUT), and ‘Technology Acceptance Model’ (TAM) to explain
the determinants of AI adoption (Dabbous et al., 2022; Kinkel et al., 2022; Getson and Nejat,
2022), there is a lack of any review paper that offers a comprehensive overview of those
determinants.
Previous review studies have examined the adoption of AI-based systems within specific
contexts. For instance, Oppioli et al. (2023) explored the implications of AI in management
decision-making, highlighting its integration across various business functions, with a
significant influence on understanding human behaviour patterns. Pietronudo et al. (2022)
analysed the influence of AI on innovative management as a decision-making process. Pramod
(2022) investigated the challenges associated with adopting robotic process automation (RPA)
systems, while Pradhananga et al. (2021) focused on the adoption challenges of robotics in the
construction industry, and Regona et al. (2022) explored AI adoption in the construction sector.
Ghandour (2021) and Wang et al. (2021) reviewed the literature on AI adoption in the banking
industry and within the context of Chinese smart cities, respectively. Furthermore, Yu et al.
(2022) conducted a review study examining the antecedents and consequences of AI adoption
in organisations from a socio-technical system theory perspective. Bawack et al. (2022)
conducted a bibliometric study to analyse and synthesise the existing literature on AI in ecommerce to determine the current state of the field. Secinaro et al. (2021) conducted a
bibliometric analysis of AI applications in healthcare aimed at identifying the key journals,
authors, and influential works, and understanding the significant themes and trends within this
area of research. Di Vaio et al. (2020) provided a detailed overview of how AI can support the
development of sustainable business models aligned with Sustainable Development Goals
(SDGs). More details about these review studies are presented in Table I. The last row of this

(1) to identify the determinants of AI adoption at firm and employee levels.
(2) to develop a conceptual model for clarifying the relationships among the determinants

(3) to synthesise the literature on AI adoption and provide directions for future studies.

2. Systematic review protocol
To achieve the objectives of this study regarding identifying and categorising the determinants
of AI adoption in organisations, a systematic review was conducted on the papers that discussed
AI adoption in organisations. The review was conducted following the ‘Preferred Reporting
Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis’ (PRISMA) guidelines (Page et al., 2021).
The PRISMA protocol is the most used guidance and explicit plan for systematic review. It
aims to minimise bias in source article selection and content analysis.
The PRISMA protocol has been widely utilised in other systematic review studies within
similar contexts of technology adoption. For example, it has been applied in studies examining
the drivers of AI adoption in the healthcare industry (Khanijahani et al., 2022), public
administration (Madan and Ashok, 2023), and hospitality industry (Rasheed et al., 2024), as
well as the factors influencing the adoption of clinical decision support systems in hospitals
(Shakibaei Bonakdeh et al., 2024), and the drivers and barriers of Industry 4.0 technology
adoption in manufacturing (Ghobakhloo et al., 2022).
2.1. Database and search terms
Search terms were used to extract papers from the Scopus database on 12 July 2022. Scopus
was selected as the largest database of peer-reviewed journals, covering a wide range of
information management journals. Moreover, the Scopus database is more comprehensive
compared to other databases, as it includes the majority of journals (Oppioli et al., 2023; Singh
et al., 2021).
To review the papers published on AI adoption, the search was conducted within the title,
abstract, and keywords of the papers (Oppioli et al., 2023) using the following search terms:
(‘artificial intelligence’ OR ‘chatbots’ OR ‘robot*’ OR ‘expert system*’ OR
‘voice*recognition’ OR ‘face*recognition’) AND (‘factor*’ OR ‘challenge*’ OR ‘barrier*’
OR ‘determinant’) AND (‘adoption’ OR ‘acceptance’ OR ‘intention to use’ OR ‘usage
intention’). These keywords defined the boundaries of this research, and the authors aimed to
ensure that all aspects of AI adoption were taken into account during the selection of keywords
for the search terms (Mishra et al., 2017; Oppioli et al., 2023).
2.2. Paper inclusion and exclusion criteria
This study applied four inclusion and exclusion criteria to screen papers and identify relevant
works. The criteria were defined as follows: (1) papers should be in English (Oppioli et al.,
2023), (2) papers should be published in journals. The use of journal articles enhances the
reliability of results, as journal articles are considered ‘certified knowledge’ (Ramos‐Rodríguez
and Ruíz‐Navarro, 2004), (3) papers should discuss the determinants of AI adoption in
organisations at either the firm or employee level. This is because the scope of this paper is to
identify the determinants of AI adoption at the firm and employee levels. In this sense, papers


discussing AI adoption at the firm and employee levels are included, while those focusing
solely on the individual (non-employee) level of AI adoption, the applications of AI, or the
adoption of technologies other than AI are excluded. To ensure that the relevant papers have
been selected, the authors reviewed and screened the papers based on the titles and abstracts.
Then, the remaining papers were reviewed based on their content in full-text (Oppioli et al.,
2023; Di Vaio et al., 2020), and (4) the full text of the identified papers should be accessible
for further analysis (Khanfar et al., 2021).
2.3. Search results
The search results are shown in Figure 1. The search was conducted on 12 July 2022, and thus
the data retrieved do not include the entirety of the year 2022. The search returned 4,775 papers
and 2,051 shortlisted papers after applying the first two inclusion criteria. The titles and
abstracts of 2,051 papers were reviewed separately and independently by the authors, resulting
in 158 papers. This result is because the authors excluded papers that are not directly related
to AI adoption in an organisational context. The authors excluded studies that investigated AI
adoption and usage from individual perspectives rather than organisational perspectives. For
example, studies that investigated AI adoption for consumers (not employees) and students
(not educators). Furthermore, the authors excluded papers that studied the applications of AI
and robotics, which are not related to AI adoption. For example, studies that investigated the
applications of AI and/or robotics in agriculture, healthcare, and marketing sectors. The authors
also excluded papers that studied the applications and/or adoption of other technologies than
AI, such as blockchain, IoT, and/or 3D printing. In the subsequent screening, 57 papers were
excluded based on the full-text screen, and 11 papers were excluded due to full-text
unavailability. Consequently, 90 papers about AI adoption in organisations were identified and
reviewed.

3. Results and findings
Previous studies have used firm-level theories and models, such as TOE, institutional theory,
and resource-based theory, to explain the determinants of technology adoption at the firm level,
whereas individual-level theories, such as TAM, UTAUT, and ECM, have been used to explain
the drivers of technology adoption at the individual level. The assumption is that the firm- and
individual-level factors are independent of each other. In this review, we challenged this
assumption to examine whether the determinants of technology adoption at the individual and
firm levels can be interrelated to derive a more holistic adoption model. We categorised the
determinants into individual, social, technological, organisational, and environmental factors.

3.1. Individual and social factors
Individual and social factors can be categorised into perception- and feeling-related factors,
personal characteristics, and social factors.
3.1.1.

Individual perceptions and feelings

The perception- and feeling-related factors and their effects on AI adoption discussed in the
literature include fear of job loss, resistance to using the AI systems, anxiety from using the AI
systems, perceived usefulness, perceived ease of use, perceived risks, perceived trust, and
perceived safety. Although automating operations and tasks is considered a benefit of AI at the
firm level (Yu et al., 2022), it may raise employees’ fear of being replaced by AI systems and
losing their jobs (Al Badi et al., 2022). Fear of job loss may lead to resistance to accepting and
using the new system (Pramod, 2022). AI-based systems may change the way employees
perform their tasks and the nature of the tasks, which may cause anxiety (Cao et al., 2021) and
resistance to adoption (Sibbald et al., 2022).


AI systems enable employees to perform their tasks efficiently, thereby improving their
productivity (Brooks et al., 2020). Employees’ perceptions and beliefs about the benefits and
usefulness of an AI system and its ability to enhance their job performance positively influence
their intention to use it (Wang et al., 2021). If the system is easy to use, employees are more
likely to use it (Vichitkraivin and Naenna, 2021). The perceived threats and risks of using an
AI system negatively influence employees’ intention to use them (Cao et al., 2021). Enhanced
privacy, security, and the ability of AI systems to protect information are other important
factors that influence employees’ acceptance of and interaction with AI systems (Pramod,
2022). However, privacy and security concerns and perceived threats/risks may destroy
employees’ trust in the AI system which in turn reduces their intention to use it (Choudhury et
al., 2022) and increases their resistance to using the system owing to their fear of sharing
personal data (Campion et al., 2022).
3.1.2.

Personal characteristics

Self-efficacy, personal innovativeness, willingness to acquire and transfer knowledge, beliefs,
development concerns, experience in using systems, ICT/AI knowledge, and ICT competence
are personal characteristics highlighted in the literature that influence the adoption of AI
systems at the individual level. Concerns about the capabilities to learn or use AI systems (selfefficacy) may influence employees’ intention to adopt and use them (Yu et al., 2022).
Employees with a higher tendency to learn new technologies may have a higher intention to
adopt AI systems (Pizam et al., 2022). On the contrary, a lack of knowledge and competence
in digital technologies and AI systems negatively influences employees’ behaviour toward
adopting AI systems (Nam et al., 2021). The willingness to acquire and transfer knowledge
positively influences AI adoption (Choi et al., 2022). Employees' perceptions of whether using
the AI system reflects their beliefs and images may also influence their intention to accept and
use the AI system (Dabbous et al., 2022). Acquiring experience and personal development are
important for some employees. AI systems may be less likely to be adopted by such employees
if they perceive that AI systems may hinder their personal development (Ghandour, 2021).
3.1.3.

Social factors

Social factors refer to the impact of social influence and the opinions of other people on a user’s
beliefs, feelings, and intention to use a technology (Vichitkraivin and Naenna, 2021).
Perceptions of social surroundings of AI systems, perceptions of social surroundings of the
individual (image), and impact on the community are social factors influencing the adoption
and use of AI systems. The perceptions of social groups regarding AI systems can influence
organisations and employees toward adopting them (Pramod, 2022). Personal relationships
between employees increase effective communication and discussions about the new system
which positively influences their intention to accept it (Tyson and Sauers, 2021). The opinions
and perceptions of colleagues and managers about AI systems (subjective norms or social
influence) influence employees’ behaviour toward using the systems (Rantanen et al., 2018).
The opinions of colleagues about the employees who use the system also affect their decision
to use it (Bröhl et al., 2019). A summary of the individual and social factors identified in the
review is presented in Table II.

3.2. Organisational factors
Organisational factors refer to organisation-related elements that influence the adoption of
technology, such as leadership and management, competencies, capabilities, and resource
availability (Phuoc, 2022). AI affects organisations at two levels; organisational and individual.
Firm-level influencing factors refer to those that affect firms’ decisions to adopt a technology.
Individual-level factors affect employees’ adoption and use of technology. Organisational
factors influencing AI adoption are listed in Table III.
3.2.1.

Firm-level influencing organisational factors

The firm-level influencing organisational factors of AI adoption are organisational readiness
(Agarwal, 2022), AI governance systems (Bedué and Fritzsche, 2022), financial readiness
(Bademosi and Issa, 2021), firm size (Golightly et al., 2022), technical readiness (Grover et
al., 2022), and reliability and accuracy concerns (Al Badi et al., 2022). Organisational readiness
refers to the degree to which an organisation is prepared to adopt a new system, and involves
its structure, resources, processes, organisational infrastructure, management readiness, and
strategy (Agarwal, 2022; Pramod, 2022). The adoption of AI systems leads to significant
changes in organisational business processes and strategic goals (Yu et al., 2022). As such,
setting an effective AI adoption strategy helps organisations efficiently utilise resources, time,
and capabilities, resulting in time savings, reduced risks, and increased performance and
perceived benefits of AI (Tjebane et al., 2022). Setting expectations and priorities at
organisations is also important as it facilitates effective collaboration, coordination, and clear
communication among employees at all levels. Thus, it is critical to ensure that clear
expectations and priorities are defined.
The organisational governance system is another important determinant for adopting AI
systems (Pramod, 2022). Data sharing is required to train AI systems to generate accurate
results and take advantage of their capabilities (Campion et al., 2022). Organisations are
required to design and develop a governance system, upgrade the current ones (Pramod, 2022),
create standardisation and a framework for sharing and using data (Regona et al., 2022), and
develop quality assurance processes to facilitate AI adoption (Pramod, 2022).
Organisation size influences organisational adoption decisions (Bademosi and Issa, 2021). The
adoption of AI requires financial fundings, which can be a challenge for small organisations
due to financial constraints (Kalyanaraman et al., 2022). Large organisations have access to
the large and high-quality data required for training AI systems (Kinkel et al., 2022). Ethical
concerns also influence AI adoption in organisations (Kalyanaraman et al., 2022). Accurate


data are needed to train AI systems in order to operate successfully and produce accurate results
(Al Badi et al., 2022). However, the accuracy of data can be impacted by many factors, such
as biased and discriminative data selection (intentional and not intentional) (Al Badi et al.,
2022; Singh et al., 2020) and accountability, and the trustworthiness of the participants in
providing information (Al Badi et al., 2022). Therefore, concerns regarding the accuracy,
efficiency, and reliability of the results may adversely influence AI adoption (Al Badi et al.,
2022; Campion et al., 2022). While the above organisational factors can be categorised based
on their effects on the firm, some organisational factors can affect AI adoption at both the firm
and individual levels. This will be discussed in the next section.
3.2.2.

Organisational factors influencing both firm- and individual-level adoption

The organisational factors of AI adoption that influence both the firm and individual levels are
the clarity of roles and responsibilities (Baumgartner et al., 2022), innovation culture
(Neumann et al., 2022), resistance to change (Priya et al., 2022), collaboration and
communication culture (Tjebane et al., 2022), availability of skilled resources/experts
(Agarwal, 2022), availability of educational resources and training (Baumgartner et al., 2022),
task complexity (Kinkel et al., 2022), effective operations (Campion et al., 2022), managerial
capabilities (Phuoc, 2022), top management support (Grover et al., 2022), and technology
implementation experience (Bademosi and Issa, 2021).
AI implementation results in changes in organisational processes; thus, it requires redefining
the roles of employees and assigning clear responsibilities. A clear definition of roles enables
employees to understand their responsibilities, which enhances their involvement and
engagement in the process of adoption and implementation (Mogaji and Nguyen, 2022). An
organisation’s innovative culture encourages the adoption of new ideas and systems. A strong
organisational innovation culture motivates employees to accept changes in how tasks are
performed using the new system (Delgado et al., 2019). Collaboration and a communication
culture are also required for new technology adoption. Through effective collaboration,
employees from different departments can share their knowledge and experiences and
contribute to a better understanding of the requirements, thereby facilitating successful
implementation (Richthofen et al., 2022).
Skilled resources and experts play an important role in the implementation process and
facilitate successful implementation of the new system (Pradhananga et al., 2021). Experts
assist employees in using the new system and provide necessary support, resulting in an
increase in employee confidence in adopting the system (BenMessaoud et al., 2011). The
availability of educational resources is another critical factor in AI adoption (Simões et al.,
2020). Awareness and training sessions enable employees to develop the necessary skills to
use new AI systems (Yu et al., 2022).
Employees may be motivated to use an AI system if they find it useful for solving complex
problems and increasing productivity (Kinkel et al., 2022). The manner in which operations
are managed and conducted with internal and external stakeholders has an impact on the
adoption and implementation of AI systems (Regona et al., 2022). Inefficient operations
increase the duration of tasks, complicate the decision-making process, and negatively impact
employees’ experiences with newly implemented systems. Employees are more likely to
perceive the new system as easy to use and beneficial when the operations are efficient
(Chatterjee, Rana, et al., 2021). Changing management practices and involving employees in
the adoption process facilitates AI adoption by enabling employees to contribute to the


adoption process from the early stages and help resolve any concerns or misconceptions about
the new system (Pramod, 2022) such as the fear of losing the job (Richthofen et al., 2022).
Managerial capabilities are also important at both the firm and individual levels in the process
of adopting AI. Management’s ability to facilitate effective communication and coordination
among employees is essential for successful implementation of AI systems. Management plays
a crucial role in establishing an innovative culture, a collaborative culture among employees,
and an efficient decision-making process (Phuoc, 2022). Consequently, employees are less
concerned with the new system and are more likely to engage with it (Kar et al., 2021).
Management support influences employees’ attitudes and intentions to use the new system
(Tjebane et al., 2022). Adopting AI systems requires strategic decisions on investments,
planning (Pizam et al., 2022), resource allocation, and organisational changes in terms of
structure and policies (Tjebane et al., 2022) and affects both the firm and individual levels.
Thus, top management support is essential for a successful AI system implementation (Pizam
et al., 2022). Moreover, top management is responsible for securing the necessary resources
(human and financial) for successful AI implementation and adoption (Yu et al., 2022). Lack
of support from top management negatively impacts the adoption and implementation of AI
systems (Pizam et al., 2022).
An organisation’s past experience in new technology implementation plays an important role
in the success of its adoption and implementation (Kinkel et al., 2022). Changes in processes,
work methods, and communication are inherent challenges when adopting new systems. The
organisation’s past experience facilitates AI implementation by contributing to the
development of knowledge, capacity, operational practices, and a performance-oriented
culture, as well as effective collaboration between employees (Tjebane et al., 2022).
Additionally, past experience contributes to the development of employees’ digital skills,
which triggers the use of technology (Kinkel et al., 2022). The lack of research and
development (R&D) limits organisational knowledge about AI systems (Huang et al., 2021).

3.3. Environmental factors
Environmental factors refer to out-of-firm elements that influence AI adoption, such as
government support, competitive markets, and customer pressure. The environmental factors
that influence AI adoption are presented in Table IV.
3.3.1.

Firm-level influencing environmental factors

The firm-level influencing environmental factors include competitive pressure, market
uncertainty, skilled resources in the market, market demand, industry and governmental
regulations, government support, political issues, AI vendor availability, and corporate social
responsibility. At the firm level, organisational adoption decisions are influenced by
competitive pressure (Pizam et al., 2022) and market uncertainty (Phuoc, 2022). These
environmental factors motivate organisations to adopt cutting-edge technologies to predict
market trends and to remain competitive (Kinkel et al., 2022). Skilled resources are important
to successfully implement and maintain AI systems and help the organisation utilise the system
efficiently. Thus, the availability of skilled resources in the market is a key determinant of an
organisation’s decision to adopt AI systems (Wang and Su, 2021). Meeting market
expectations is an essential component of an organisation’s decision to adopt a new system.
Organisations adopt AI systems to improve services, products, or communication with
customers and meet customer expectations (Dora et al., 2021).
Industry regulations (Bademosi and Issa, 2021) and government regulations (Kumar et al.,
2021) play an important role in the adoption of AI systems. Governmental regulations include
ethics, legal policies (Kumar et al., 2021), privacy and security regulations (Yu et al., 2022),
and economic governmental regulations (Chen et al., 2021). Government regulations can play
a positive role in encouraging organisations to adopt cutting-edge technologies by removing
barriers to adoption and supporting the adoption of new technologies. Security, privacy, and
ethical concerns may be addressed by regulations (Phuoc, 2022). Another factor influencing
AI adoption in organisations is AI vendors (Al Badi et al., 2022). AI systems can be
successfully adopted if reliable vendors are available and effective and transparent
relationships exist between the organisation and vendors (Singh et al., 2020).

3.3.2.

Environmental factors that influence at both the Firm-level and individual-levels

The environmental factors that affect both firm- and individual-level AI adoption include AI
vendor support and business partner support. Firms prefer to rely on dynamic vendors, who
provide continuous updates that enable their AI systems to perform more effectively.
Continuous updates, developments, and system maintenance are essential for operational
stability (Al Badi et al., 2022). The business partners of an organisation also influence AI
adoption decisions. Suppliers, IT vendors, and/or other agencies such as research centres and
financial institutions may pressure organisations to adopt new technologies (Simões et al.,


2020). Support from business partners enhances the performance of organisations and allows
them to acquire knowledge and experience. Business partners also provide support to
employees and assist them in understanding the benefits and utility of AI systems. Thus,
business partners contribute to the AI adoption process at both the firm and individual levels
(Chatterjee, Rana, et al., 2021).

3.4. Technological factors


Technological factors refer to the technological components and capabilities that impact AI
decision-making in organisations (Phuoc, 2022). Table V presents the technological factors
associated with AI adoption. This Table also maps each factor with its level of influence,
namely, the technological and/or individual influence level.
3.4.1.

Firm-level influencing technological factors

The firm-level influencing technological factors include return on investment (Baumgartner et
al., 2022), cost of AI systems (Law et al., 2022), benefits of AI systems (Agarwal, 2022),
technology integration (Kalyanaraman et al., 2022), compatibility with IT infrastructure (Na et
al., 2022), system maturity (Golightly et al., 2022), and system reliability and accuracy
(Campion et al., 2022). AI adoption imposes various costs on organisations, such as
implementation, operation, and maintenance costs (Pramod, 2022; Bademosi and Issa, 2021).
The implementation of artificial intelligence may pose a financial challenge for organisations,
as it may require companies to consider implementing new business models and technologies
and upgrading the existing IT infrastructure or building a new infrastructure, which requires
significant financial investments (Kalyanaraman et al., 2022). The costs associated with
adoption, such as system and implementation costs, must also be covered (Tjebane et al., 2022).
Therefore, the adoption and implementation costs of AI systems play a significant role in an
organisation's decision to adopt them (Pillai and Sivathanu, 2020).
AI systems help businesses increase productivity, reduce costs, optimise operational
performance, and speed up organisational processes (Chen et al., 2021). A company's decision
to adopt an AI system is influenced by its potential benefits (Chen et al., 2021). Moreover, AI
adoption requires integration of an AI system with an organisation’s existing technologies
(Kalyanaraman et al., 2022). Compatibility of the new AI system with the current infrastructure
is an important factor in adoption decisions (Phuoc, 2022). The maturity of a new system
influences organisational adoption decisions. There is concern among organisations regarding
the risks associated with artificial intelligence systems (Kinkel et al., 2022). Adopting and
using AI in organisations raises concerns related to the precision of results (Singh et al., 2020).
Mature AI systems are more accurate because they have better decision-making capabilities
and predictability. Thus, the reliability and accuracy of a system are important factors in the
adoption process (Kar et al., 2021) where organisations prefer to adopt well-known, reliable,
and widely adopted systems with proven records (Kinkel et al., 2022).

3.4.2.

Technological factors at Firm-level and individual-level factors

The firm- and individual-level influencing technological factors include system complexity
(Du and Gao, 2022), privacy and security (Al Badi et al., 2022), trust in technology (Campion
et al., 2022), and task-technology fit (Pizam et al., 2022). The complexity of the system and its
implementation are important aspects to be considered by organisations (Pramod, 2022). A
complex AI system incurs additional implementation costs and time (Phuoc, 2022). The
complexity of a system also affects employee behaviour toward the new system. If a system’s
features and interfaces are not complex, they are easy to use and more likely to be accepted by
employees. The complexity of the system may also negatively influence employees’ intention
to use the new system because of concerns about the difficulty of learning and using it (Yu et
al., 2022).
Adopting and using AI in organisations may raise privacy and security concerns (Singh et al.,
2020). Information privacy and the risks associated with data sharing impact AI adoption and


usage decisions and may affect the level of trust in AI (Brooks et al., 2020). The data used by
AI systems can be sensitive and can contain organisational and personal information about
users or customers. Therefore, firms are concerned about the security measures that should be
taken to protect their data against hacks and cyber-attacks that technology may allow. Trust in
technology affects employees’ attitudes toward the AI system and influences their decisions to
use it (Choi et al., 2022).
Adopting new AI systems may require the modification of business models and processes to
successfully implement and use new systems and achieve business objectives (Pizam et al.,

organisations can make informed decisions regarding how to integrate AI into their operations
to achieve the desired results and benefits (Campion et al., 2022). This may also influence
employee behaviour toward using an AI system. When an AI system does not fit the
requirements of employees’ tasks, employees are required to exert additional effort to use the
features of the system. By contrast, if the characteristics of the system are aligned with
employees’ tasks, the system is more effective and user friendly (Pillai and Sivathanu, 2020).

4.

Discussion and implications

This study reviewed the literature on AI adoption and identified the factors that influence AI
adoption in organisations. As discussed in the previous sections, the factors are categorised into
individual, social, organisational, environmental, and technological factors. Accordingly, this
study proposes a model that explains the determinants of AI adoption at the firm (blue) and
employee (yellow) levels (see Figure 3). In this model, we challenge the assumption that the
factors determining AI adoption at the firm and employee levels are independent. We divided
the TOE factors into firm-level and firm- and individual-level influencing factors. The former
factors (the blue and yellow boxes within the right blue brace, which include both firm- and
individual-level factors) only influence the decision to adopt AI systems at the firm level
(managers’ decision to invest). These factors only affect the decisions of managers to invest in
an AI system, and not the decisions of employees to use it. The firm- and individual-level
influencing factors influence the decision of managers and employees to invest in and utilise
AI systems. The factors presented in Figure 3 are discussed in detail here.
4.1.Implication #1. AI adoption from technological perspective
The return on investment and cost of AI systems are two examples of firm-level influencing
technological factors. Organisations must carefully assess the benefits of integrating AI
systems into their operations, as the adoption process necessitates significant investments in
both time and money. Therefore, it is important for them to ensure that the chosen AI system
is not only useful but also justifies the investment (Baumgartner et al., 2022). Additionally, the
successful integration of the AI system with the organisation's existing technological
infrastructure plays a significant role in managers’ decisions (Kalyanaraman et al., 2022).
Organisations must thoroughly evaluate the compatibility and integration potential of the new


AI system with their current infrastructure (Kumar and Kalse, 2021), as these factors directly
impact costs and investment decisions. Return on investment and the cost of AI systems are
important only to managers. Employees' decision to use AI systems is not affected by these
factors, and accordingly, they are considered to be firm-level influencing technological factors.
However, there are some technological factors, such as system complexity and security, that
both managers and employees consider in making decisions regarding AI system adoption.
Besides managers, employees are also concerned about the privacy and ease of use of AI
systems, and these concerns significantly influence their decision to use AI. Thus, these factors
are firm- and individual-level influencing technological factors. Therefore, some technological
factors not only influence a firm’s decision, but also have a significant influence on the decision
of its employees to adopt AI (Figure 3).
4.2.Implication #2. AI adoption from organisational perspective
There are various organisational firm-level and firm- and individual-level factors that influence
decisions regarding the adoption of new AI systems. Organisations need both the capability
and willingness to adopt these systems. Factors such as financial capability, size, technical
readiness, availability of technical resources, and the presence of proper AI governance and
strategy all play a role in managers’ decision-making process at the firm level. Additionally,
there exist various firm- and employee-level organisational factors such as top management
support and innovation culture (Neumann et al., 2022; Simões et al., 2020). Top management
support influences both managers and employees. Management support is crucial in adopting
AI systems as they need to allocate a budget for that. Furthermore, employees' willingness to
accept and use the AI system depends on management support in terms of providing training
and resources for successful implementation. Another factor influencing the decision to adopt
AI in organisations and by employees is the extent of innovation culture. An environment that
encourages creative thinking and the implementation of new ideas is vital. The existence of an
innovation culture positively motivates employees to engage with the changes resulting from
new technologies, facilitating the adoption process and thus convincing management to invest
in AI systems.
4.3.Implication #3. AI adoption from environmental perspective
Environmental factors at the firm level also play a significant role in an organisation's decision
to adopt a new AI system. External pressures imposed on organisations, arising from
competition within the industry, industry regulations (Dora et al., 2021), the availability of
skilled resources in the market (Wang and Su, 2021), AI vendor support, as well as
governmental considerations such as regulations, government support (Phuoc, 2022), social
responsibility policies (Wang and Su, 2021), and political issues (Kumar et al., 2021), all
influence an organisation's decision-making process regarding AI adoption. Although some of
these factors, such as industry regulations, only affect managers' decisions at the firm level,
others, such as AI vendor support, they also impact employees' decisions. Employees are
influenced by the availability of support from AI vendors and business partners, as it provides
them with the necessary assistance in using the new system. AI vendor supports and business
partner supports have a substantial impact on AI adoption at both the firm and individual levels.
4.4.Implication #4. AI adoption from employee and social perspectives
The study categorised individual factors into individual perceptions and feelings, and personal
characteristics. Individual and social factors influence employee intention to adopt AI systems.
Employee perceptions and feelings significantly impact AI adoption. AI systems possess


remarkable capabilities for performing tasks, potentially raising concerns among employees
about the threat of job displacement and job loss (Baumgartner et al., 2022). These concerns
can lead to a sense of risk and resistance to AI adoption. Furthermore, factors like the ease of
using AI systems and their overall usefulness play pivotal roles in shaping employee attitudes
and behaviours towards AI adoption (Choi et al., 2022). Additionally, the potential risks
associated with privacy and safety also influence employees' trust in AI systems, subsequently
affecting their willingness to adopt AI technologies (Choudhury et al., 2022).
Personal characteristics may moderate the influence of other factors on AI system adoption at
an employee level. For instance, perceived ease of use is expected to be less important for
employees with a greater tendency to learn new technologies. Social factors may directly or
indirectly influence the adoption of AI systems at the employee level. Social factors indirectly
influence intention to adopt through individual perceptions and feelings (Figure 3). Tables IIV provide a list of firm- and individual-level influencing factors, social factors, individual
perceptions and feelings, and personal characteristics that may affect AI adoption at both the
firm and individual levels.

5. Theoretical and practical contributions
5.1.Theoretical contribution
This study contributes to the literature in several ways. First, this review synthesised the
findings of previous studies and highlights the determinants of AI adoption at both the firm and
employee levels. These findings provide a comprehensive view of the determinants of the
adoption of AI. We categorised these determinants into social, individual, and TOE factors.
Second, we extended the literature by showing that TOE factors also influence employees’
decisions to adopt AI systems. The study categorised TOE factors into firm- and individuallevel influencing factors. Both firm- and individual-level factors affect firms’ decisions to
adopt AI. Individual-level influencing factors may shape employees’ perceptions and feelings
toward AI systems and, in turn, lead to their intention to adopt them. Third, the study proposed
a model of the association between TOE, personal and social factors, and adoption at the
individual and firm levels. This model can be used in future studies. Finally, the study identified
gaps in the literature on the determinants of AI adoption and proposed directions for future
studies.
5.2. Practical contribution
AI systems have the potential to support managerial decisions and optimise the strategic
decision-making process made by managers. However, human behaviours toward new AI
systems, managers' competencies, and the perceived ease of use and usefulness play a
significant role in adopting AI systems in organisations (Oppioli et al., 2023). This study
contributes to practice in several ways by exploring the determinants of AI adoption and
extending managers' understanding of AI adoption in organisations. First, an understanding of
the factors that should be considered in the adoption stage allows managers to make informed
decisions, considering their firms’ resources and capabilities, as well as their business
environment. Additionally, managers can successfully implement AI systems by understanding
the determinants of their employees’ attitudes and behaviours toward AI systems. For instance,
knowing that system complexity may lead to employee resistance during implementation may
result in avoiding the adoption of complex software. It also may encourage managers to
develop comprehensive training programs and establish continuous support mechanisms,
ensuring that employees feel supported in their AI adoption journey. Furthermore, AI system
designers understand the importance of designing user interfaces that are intuitive and userfriendly. Second, this review enables managers to understand the effects of organisational,
environmental, and technological factors on employees’ intention to use AI systems.
Identifying the role of TOE factors, along with personal and social factors, enables firms to
develop effective managerial practices to facilitate AI adoption and implementation within
their organisations. Organisations can adopt different managerial practices to deal with
adoption challenges and barriers and facilitate the adoption process. Managerial practices
include providing support to the employees, training, and educational resources, establishing
an innovation and collaboration culture to motivate employees accept and use the new systems,
defining clear roles and responsibilities, and ensuring that the skilled resources (expertise) can
provide adequate support for the employees.
6. Limitations and directions for future research


This review is subject to certain constraints that ought to be acknowledged. First, the types of
selected articles were limited to journal articles. The use of journal articles enhances the
reliability of the results, as they are considered 'certified knowledge'. However, future research
may want to explore other types of publications such as conference proceedings, reports, and
books. Second, the Scopus database was chosen for article collection due to its extensive size.
Nonetheless, subsequent research should explore additional databases like ProQuest, Web of
Science, and EBSCO to broaden the analysis and enhance the results.
Further, this study opens doors for future research arenas in the adoption literature. The review
of studies on the determinants of AI adoption in organisations enabled us to identify several
research gaps and recommend future research directions.
•

Prior studies on AI adoption have primarily focused on the factors that influence AI
adoption at the firm level. However, less attention has been paid to the factors that
influence employee intention to adopt AI systems. As a substantial proportion of AI
adoption projects fail at the implementation stage (Makarius et al., 2020), future studies
are required to explore the determinants of employees’ intention to use AI systems.

•

As in previous studies on technology adoption at the individual level, few studies on
AI adoption have used individual-level theories, such as TAM and UTAUT to explain
the determinants of AI adoption at the employee level. Our review demonstrates that
TOE factors may also influence employees’ perceptions and feelings, which in turn
may influence their decisions regarding the use of AI systems. Future studies are
recommended to study the influence of TOE factors on employees’ perceptions,
feelings, attitudes, and behaviours.

•

The influence of employee perceptions and feelings on their intention to adopt AI
systems has been proven in the literature. However, studies have assumed that
employees are homogenous and that the influences of factors are similar among them.
In reality, the characteristics and needs of employees differ, and the factors influencing
their adoption decisions are not similar. Accordingly, future studies should consider the
heterogeneities among employees and test the moderating influences of personal
characteristics, such as self-efficacy, personal innovativeness, and knowledge-sharing
tendency.

•

Most studies on the determinants of AI adoption assume that the AI adoption decision
is rational and is influenced by functional factors such as ease of use and usefulness.
However, hedonic and social factors play important roles in individuals’ adoption
decisions. Accordingly, it is recommended that future research assess the role of
hedonic and social factors, such as perceived enjoyment and social interaction, in
addition to functional factors.

•

The factors influencing AI adoption do not exist in isolation and are expected to interact
with each other. Moreover, certain factors may dominate others, depending on various
contexts such as the organisation, industry, and social environment. To address this gap
in understanding the interactions among factors influencing AI adoption, we propose
the following directions for future research that help addressing the complexities and
interdependencies of the AI adoption factors:
o Future studies should aim to quantitatively assess the impact of each factor on
AI adoption. Understanding the strength of these impacts is crucial for both


scholars and practitioners. For this purpose, Artificial Neural Network (ANN)
approach is recommended, as it allows to rank the factors based on their level
of importance.
o Future research is recommended to prioritise the factors based on their influence
on AI adoption. This prioritisation will provide valuable insights for decisionmakers in formulating strategies. The Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) is an
effective method for such ranking, as it involves expert judgments to evaluate
the relative importance of each factor.
o To understand how different combinations of factors influence AI adoption,
future studies are required to explore and uncover complex interaction patterns.
Fuzzy-Set Qualitative Comparative Analysis (fsQCA) is recommended for this
purpose, as it allows for the examination of how different factor combinations
lead to varying outcomes.
o Future studies need to demonstrate the interrelations between factors, as this
will provide a comprehensive understanding of the AI adoption process, aiding
both scholars and managers in decision-making. Multi-Criteria Decision
Making (MCDM) approaches such as the Adaptive Neuro-Fuzzy Inference
System (ANFIS) are recommended. ANFIS is particularly suitable for
modelling complex, non-linear interrelationships, combining the advantages of
neural networks and fuzzy logic.
7.

Conclusion

The primary purpose of this study was to synthesise the literature on the determinants of AI
adoption and provide directions for future studies. To address this study’s objectives, 90
research papers on the determinants of AI adoption were reviewed. The study categorised AI
adoption influencing factors into individual, social, technological, organisational, and
environmental factors. As shown in Tables III to V, firm-level factors also influence
employees’ behaviours toward AI systems, which we call individual-level TOE-influencing
factors. The impact of these factors on employees’ perceptions, feelings, and behaviours toward
new technologies requires further investigation. This study proposed an AI adoption model that
demonstrates the potential associations between individual and social, organisational,
environmental, and technological factors. Further studies are required to assess this model in
different contexts. The study highlights drivers and barriers to AI adoption at the individual
and firm levels and provides directions for firm managers.
