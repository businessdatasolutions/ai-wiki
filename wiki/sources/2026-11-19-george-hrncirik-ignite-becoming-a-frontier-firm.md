# George & Hrncirik (Microsoft Ignite 2026) — Microsoft's learnings becoming a Frontier Firm

> Microsoft has learned firsthand that deploying AI isn't the same as transforming with it. Drawing on Microsoft's Customer Zero journey, Katy George shares the lessons and recipes helping Microsoft redesign work, build new capabilities, measure impact, and scale what works. Leave with a practical playbook for applying those lessons to your own AI transformation.

## TL;DR

A 46-minute breakout session at Microsoft Ignite 2026 (BRK531, level 100 "Foundational"), published 19 November 2026 on the Microsoft Events channel. **Katy George**, Microsoft CVP Workforce and AI Transformation, presents for 22 minutes. She then holds a fireside chat with **Cory Hrncirik**, Microsoft Senior Director Finance, and opens the floor. Both titles are from the session's title slide. It is the slide-led, numbered version of what George described on [[2026-07-27-george-wood-worklab-future-of-work-has-no-org-chart|WorkLab in July 2026]]. Five claims:

1. **A Frontier Firm is "a new organizational blueprint for companies that are AI-powered but human-led"** (slide). Microsoft is *"in the process, just like you, of getting to what we call the frontier,"* with 50 years of legacy systems, data, processes and *"professional identities."*
2. **Value comes in four areas, named on the Frontier Firm Success Framework:** enrich employee experiences, reinvent customer engagement, reshape business processes, bend the curve on innovation. The employees who use AI most *"report the highest thrive scores."*
3. **Start with business goals, not technology.** *"If you start with a tech, you get some interesting little pilots that do not scale."* There is no company-wide ROI metric: in sales it is revenue per seller, in finance efficiency, accuracy and internal experience. She names **two cardinal sins**: automating a bad process, and stopping at automation (*"you make your company a little smaller, maybe a little faster, but not better"*). Microsoft finds *"more impact on the top line than we're finding even on the bottom line."*
4. **Three recipes**, distilled by a **Customer Zero Council** from **100+ internal case studies** and published as the *Microsoft Frontier Playbook*. Persona acceleration (sales), AI-powered process redesign (supply chain, finance), AI-first possibility (the Camp AIR bootcamp, the Copilot Cowork build). Recipes 4 and 5, *"more agentic kind of approaches to redesign,"* are in progress.
5. **Finance scaled by focus and reuse.** In spring 2023 the team turned 130 ideas into about 12 business cases, and CFO Amy Hood picked six. A shared document intelligence platform let it deliver 16 gen AI projects in year one instead of the six promised. Today it runs six end-to-end processes with KPIs, a central pro-code team plus citizen developers, *"hundreds and hundreds of AI solutions"* and 4,000 people in finance using them.

## Key claims

### Tech, process and people in one team

When George's team began, *"about a year and a half ago,"* it formed the **Customer Zero Council**. Its members are the people who lead transformation in each business function of the commercial business, plus engineers. The team's job is the *"connective tissue"*: how finance learns from supply chain, and supply chain from sales. It analysed *"100 different situations across Microsoft where we had transformed our own work with AI,"* what worked and what did not, and published the patterns as the **Frontier Playbook**.

The first learning: business impact at scale, rather than *"little pockets of innovation that don't connect,"* came where engineers worked with people who understand process. *"You'll sometimes hear us talk about continuous improvement as a foundational skill for our AI transformation."* These are the lean tools, *"which frankly, we'd never done with knowledge work before."* The third leg is people: changing *"their behaviors, their mindsets, often their professional identity."*

### Recipe 1: persona acceleration (sales)

The goal is revenue per seller, through more time with customers and *"more insight, more ideas"* in each meeting. The first Copilot launch to sales failed. It ran as *"just another technology tool"* with the usual training play, and in the Harvard Business School case on it *"Copilot usage going up, and then crashing down to nothing,"* because sellers did not see the link to their own goals. The second attempt:

- **Ride-alongs.** Engineers, change managers and sales-cycle experts did a *"week in the life"* analysis of the account executive and built best-practice prompts and agents for it.
- **Huddles.** Groups of 15 in the same role, led by a peer power user, meet weekly: *"what did you do with AI last week? What are you planning to do next week?"* When a steering-committee member proposed stopping them, the rest answered that this *"is now rhythm of business,"* because the models and products keep changing.

What makes change management work, in her words: *"It's practitioner-led. It's in the flow of work immediately, not some training program."* She reports *"almost 10% in revenue per seller"* and much higher win rates where adoption rose. The slide is more precise: 9.4% revenue per head and 20% higher close rates *within the pilot group*, and a 3x adoption increase *as reported by survey participants*. The measurement runs as a chain: adoption, then behaviour (more customer time), then early indicators (win rates, qualified pipeline), then revenue per rep, *"which often happens over a longer period of time given our sales cycles."*

### Recipe 2: AI-powered process redesign (supply chain)

Supply chain has *"over 100 agents in use"* and a 75% reduction in time. The slide says *"in cycle time across selected workflows"*. The team mapped the end-to-end process with gemba walks, value-stream maps and kaizens. People found that they *"didn't even realize that they were all part of the same end-to-end process."* Making work visible matters because *"knowledge work is tacit. It's in our heads."* The process was leaned out first. Then agents took work out of it, and AI added capabilities the process never had.

### Recipe 3: AI-first possibility (Camp AIR, Copilot Cowork)

The third recipe is *"not grounded in the current process."* **Camp AIR** is a three-week immersive bootcamp for whole teams of engineers, designers and product managers. *"This is not where you bring your new people to the party"*: it is for people who know what excellent output looks like. Day one: *"leave your discipline at the door… You're all here as builders."* The hard part is identity, for people who built careers as *"a master craftsperson in coding"* and are told *"now you never have to code again."* From week two they apply the tools to their real projects, and each camp's emerging power users coach the next one. For the software teams that launched Cowork and Autopilot, she reports *"breakthrough kind of performance… AI native kind of performance."* The figures for the Copilot Cowork build appear only on the slides (stills 12 and 13 below).

### The finance fireside chat

Hrncirik's first discovery was *"how many Excel spreadsheets are used to run finance departments,"* and how well the people closest to the work know it. The team picked a few areas, because finance has too many sub-processes and other finance leaders get stuck in *"analysis paralysis."* The six end-to-end processes *"are not your org chart."* Quote-to-cash runs across several finance teams and beyond finance. Parts of it are still owned locally: intelligent collections belongs to Treasury and is *"a whole portfolio of AI solutions."* George cites Satya Nadella on a move *"from org charts to work charts,"* and expects organisations to work *"much more along these end-to-end process lines"*, with the matrix mattering less. Roles become *"more T-shaped."*

On scale, Hrncirik describes the spring-2023 funnel and the reuse that turned six projects into 16. *"A translation or an extraction or a summarization skill is basically universal across any document type."* What surprised him:

- the pace at which people took up the tools (*"All of our best ideas, all of them come from people closest to the work"*);
- the infrastructure it needed: *"We weren't totally prepared for the kind of onslaught"* of use cases, and had to work out how to capture, unify and share them.

George adds that simple recognition, such as Microsoft's AI Pioneers programme, is *"more than enough"* to get power users to share.

### From the floor

- **Enablement.** One participant ran focus groups and follow-up, and ended with champions. George: giving a team three weeks out of its schedule for Camp AIR is *"going slow to go fast."* Hrncirik puts *"a forward deployed engineer"* next to citizen builders whose prototype looks worth scaling.
- **A starfish model.** Each function decides its own use cases, and a centralised backlog shows what everyone is building. A new entry is checked against the other backlogs. Requests that recur across backlogs revealed *"a messy value stream"* worth redesigning end to end.
- **Scarcity, competition, low barriers.** A healthcare firm started with 300 Copilot licences, promoted them heavily, and rotated away unused ones. It ran small pockets on enterprise OpenAI and Claude in parallel, later moved its main tool to OpenAI, and kept licence requests easy but not automatic.
- **A permanent team.** Korn Ferry formed a permanent AI strategy and transformation team, drawn from all its solution areas, that runs experiments on service delivery. George: rigorous experimentation means *"you kill things that don't work. You kill them really fast, and you scale things that do work,"* inside *"very clear risk boundaries,"* and that is a skill for *"the management ranks,"* not a few people.
- **Linking KPIs to efficiency.** A bank cannot connect software-development signals to its efficiency goals. George points to the chain of adoption, behaviour change, early indicators and then efficiency, *"which I won't pretend we've really scaled across every part of Microsoft yet,"* used in a pilot until there is confidence to scale.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`.** Work is organised around six end-to-end processes that *"are not your org chart"*, quote-to-cash crosses finance and other functions, and George expects a move *"from org charts to work charts."* The Copilot Cowork squad replaces separate disciplines with nine "meta" roles working with agents.
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`.** Reskilling is done in the flow of work: role-based huddles led by power users, the three-week Camp AIR, and *"leave your discipline at the door."* It is also measured, as a chain from adoption through behaviour to revenue per rep.
- **`digital-seizing/rapid-prototyping`.** The finance funnel from 130 ideas to 12 business cases to six projects, the pilot-then-scale rule for KPIs, and the norm to *"kill them really fast, and you scale things that do work"* inside clear risk boundaries.
- **`contextual/internal-enablers`.** Business-leader ownership of goals (a CFO who picks six projects), a Customer Zero Council as connective tissue, recognition through AI Pioneers, and the infrastructure Hrncirik found he needed to capture and share use cases.
