# IBM Institute for Business Value, 2025 CEO Study

## TL;DR

The IBM-sponsored CEO survey for 2025, run by the IBM Institute for Business Value (IBV) with Oxford Economics. 2,000 CEOs, 33 geographies, February to April 2025. Launched 6 May 2025.

1. **A quarter of AI initiatives paid off.** *"Over the past three years, CEOs say only 25% of AI initiatives have delivered expected ROI—and only 16% have scaled enterprise-wide"* (PDF p. 27, printed p. 50). The unit is **initiatives**, not organisations.
2. **Most CEOs are still piloting generative AI.** Asked for the main aim of their generative-AI investment, **60%** say piloting and experimentation, 22% efficiency and cost savings, **7%** growth and expansion, 11% not investing (PDF p. 19, printed p. 35). A year earlier, CEOs expected only 20% to still be piloting by now.
3. **Value beyond cost is a coin toss.** Only **52%** say their organisation is realising value from generative AI beyond cost reduction (PDF pp. 27-28, printed pp. 51-53).
4. **Investment is speeding up anyway.** Expected two-year growth in generative-AI investment rises from 15% (2024-2026) to 31% (2025-2027); traditional AI from 13% to 31% (PDF p. 18, printed p. 32). **61%** say they are actively adopting AI agents and preparing to scale them (PDF p. 5, printed p. 7).
5. **Data is the weak spot.** 50% say the pace of recent investment has left them with *"disconnected, piecemeal technology"*; 68% call an integrated enterprise-wide data architecture critical (PDF p. 22, printed pp. 40-41).

## Method and definitions

- **Sample:** 2,000 *"CEOs or the most senior executives"* from 33 geographies and 24 industries, surveyed in the first quarter of 2025, with CEO interviews from February to April 2025 (PDF pp. 2, 37). At least 500 respondents come from companies also in IBM's parallel COO and CMO surveys. The sample is 68% publicly traded and 32% privately held, excluding government (PDF p. 38).
- **Geography:** the United States has 240 respondents (12%), the largest country group; **the Netherlands has 40 (2%)**. Europe is 470 (24%) (PDF pp. 37-38).
- **Questions:** more than 150, on innovation, technology, leadership, partnerships and talent (PDF p. 4). The question behind the 25% and 16% is not printed.
- **Unit of the headline figures.** The 25% and 16% are shares of **AI initiatives**, as estimated by CEOs over three years. They are not shares of CEOs or of organisations. The report does not say whether they are averages of each CEO's estimate or something else.
- **Analysis:** descriptive statistics, cross-tabulations, regression, and a two-step clustering of organisations on six variables tied to digital transformation (PDF p. 37). The clusters are not reported as a maturity share.
- The 14% average AI ROI on the same page comes from a separate IBM study of Chief AI Officers (endnote 4), not from this survey.

## Scope and reliability

Self-report by CEOs. IBM sells AI, hybrid cloud and consulting, and its CEO and Vice Chairman write the foreword; the five "mindshifts" are advice from a vendor. Oxford Economics ran the fieldwork. The sample is large, the country and industry breakdown is published, and repeated questions are tracked year on year. The report is candid about slow progress: it reports that CEOs' own forecasts from 2024 were far too optimistic about leaving the pilot phase.

A newer edition exists. The **2026 CEO Study** (IBM newsroom release of 4 May 2026; 2,000 CEOs and equivalent leaders, 33 geographies, 21 industries, February to April 2026) reports that 76% of organisations have a Chief AI Officer, up from 26% in 2025, and that "AI-first" organisations have scaled 10% more AI initiatives enterprise-wide. Its release does not repeat the 25% and 16%. It was not acquired.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: 65% of CEOs say they choose AI use cases on ROI, and only 16% of initiatives scaled. The report's advice is *"Focus less on quantity and more on quality"* (PDF p. 29).
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: the integrated data architecture that 68% call critical, against the piecemeal technology that 50% report.
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`**: CEOs rank organisational silos, aversion to risk and disruption, and lack of expertise as the top three barriers to innovation (PDF p. 18, printed p. 32).
