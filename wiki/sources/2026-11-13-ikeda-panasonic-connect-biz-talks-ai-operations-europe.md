## From supplier to solution provider (2:40–9:51)

*"Customer needs a measurable outcomes, not just hardware products"* (4:01), so the vision is *"to position Panasonic Connect Europe as a technology solution provider… This isn't about reinventing the business. It's about building our strength by bringing together hardware, software, and service."* He names two existing offers. **TOUGHBOOK mobile IT as a service** bundles devices, software and managed service in a subscription. In his words it will *"reduce upfront investment, provide predictable costs"* and simplify device life-cycle management. A new **Budapest tech center** provides training, maintenance and repair for manufacturers in central and eastern Europe. The company is adding consulting, system integration, software development and managed services: *"we are evolving from being technology supplier to being trusted partner."*

This is `strategic-renewal/business-model` in its plainest form: a hardware seller moving to recurring service revenue and a relationship over the customer's whole journey. [[2025-10-16-weill-cisr-business-models-ai-era|MIT CISR's AI-era business-model framework]] describes revenue moving from products and services toward customer outcomes; Ikeda's offers, as described, are subscriptions and services, with no outcome-based pricing mentioned.

His lesson from Singapore, where he worked with government on biometric authentication, sensing and wireless devices: *"customer don't buy technology alone, they buy outcomes."*

## AI and Europe (9:51–13:41)

AI is *"a foundational technology"* for labour shortage, operational complexity and *"growing the volume of data that must be acted on quickly."* Internally it helps staff *"work more efficiently, make better decision."* In products it appears in smart-factory applications for quality and productivity and in TOUGHBOOK devices for *"realtime insight at the edge."* His claim of difference: *"the ability to combine AI with real operational environments and the frontline data."* [[2026-07-30-hines-pierce-mckinsey-ai-physical-world-more-valuable|Hines-Pierce (McKinsey Podcast)]] places AI's value in physical operations from the real-estate side and names a shortage of electricians and engineers.

He says Europe can lead in AI *"because of its strong industrial basis, engineering expertise and ongoing digital investment,"* if AI is turned into *"practical outcomes"* in manufacturing, logistics and critical infrastructure. His growth areas are public safety, critical infrastructure, *"the industrial foundation of AI"* and supply chain and logistics. His advice to European leaders: *"focus on outcomes not technology alone,"* understand operations, use data better and build the right partnerships. See [[industry-4-0]].
