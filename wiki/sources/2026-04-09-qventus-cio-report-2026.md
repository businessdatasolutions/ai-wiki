# Beyond the pilot: How CIOs are operationalizing AI across health systems in 2026 (Qventus)

## TL;DR

The second annual CIO report from Qventus, a vendor of AI care-operations automation. It rests on a survey and one-to-one interviews with **more than 60** CIOs, chief AI officers, CMIOs and other senior IT leaders at medium and large US health systems (PDF p. 3). Five findings:

1. **Few have scaled.** **42%** are actively deploying AI across several use cases, but only **4%** have *"achieved scaled AI implementation with measurable outcomes."* 45% name difficulty scaling pilots as a top obstacle (p. 5).
2. **ROI is hard to measure.** Four in five struggle to determine or measure the ROI of AI. 74% need ROI within a year to justify an investment, but 38% do not expect it for 13 months or more (p. 5–6).
3. **EHR dependency is the top barrier.** **74%** say reliance on their EHR vendor's AI roadmap is a top obstacle. Asked to choose between waiting 18 months for an EHR feature and a proven third-party tool in three months, 22% would wait, down from 52% in the 2025 survey (p. 6, p. 10).
4. **Vendor sprawl, and a wish for one partner.** 51% spend 11–25% of IT bandwidth on vendor management and integration; 72% would prefer one comprehensive AI partner, but 11% work that way today (p. 9).
5. **Agents: mostly wait and see.** 57% describe their stance on agentic AI as "healthy skepticism," 34% are investing, and fewer than 6% have live deployments with measurable ROI. Yet **55%** say some AI initiatives already run *"with limited oversight from staff"* (p. 8).

## Method and definitions

- **Sample.** "More than 60" senior technology leaders: CIOs, chief AI officers, CMIOs and others, at medium and large health systems; the press release adds "U.S." (p. 3; release). No breakdown by role, size or region.
- **Method.** A survey plus one-to-one interviews (p. 3). Fieldwork dates, question wording, response options and the number answering each question are not given.
- **Definitions.** "Scaled AI implementation with measurable outcomes" is not defined further; neither is "actively deploying." "Limited oversight from staff" is not defined either.
- **Sponsor.** Qventus, which sells an "AI teammate" platform for care operations (p. 13). The report recommends risk-sharing contracts, consolidation onto one platform partner and "automated care operations" (p. 12), which is the category Qventus sells.
- **Previous edition.** A first report appeared in June 2025; it is not ingested. The report compares one question with it (EHR waiting, p. 10).

## Scope and reliability

- **Vendor research, small N.** With about 60 respondents, the 4% is two or three health systems. No margins of error.
- **Recommendations follow the seller's product.** The survey questions (one partner versus several, waiting for the EHR versus a third party) frame the choice Qventus competes in.
- **An internal inconsistency.** The share without a clear benchmarking process is "a quarter" on p. 2 and 39% on p. 5.
- **Two different 4%s.** Besides the 4% that have scaled, another 4% say they have "the appropriate resources and processes" for vendor management (p. 9). Quote the scaling figure from p. 5.
- **Borrowed context.** Several framing figures come from other sources: McKinsey's 88% of organisations using AI (p. 3) and a 2025 MIT report's "95% of generative AI pilots failed," cited via MLQ.ai (p. 4).

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: the report is about scaling up from pilots (42% deploying, 4% scaled) and about balancing internal and external options: wait for the EHR vendor's feature or buy a third-party tool now (p. 10).
- **`digital-transforming/navigating-innovation-ecosystems`**: health systems manage four to seven AI vendors at once (25%) and say they want one comprehensive partner (72%) (p. 9).
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`**: IT bandwidth spent on vendors, "tech fatigue" among staff (87%) and the inability to measure ROI (80%) hold back scaling (p. 5, p. 9, p. 11).
