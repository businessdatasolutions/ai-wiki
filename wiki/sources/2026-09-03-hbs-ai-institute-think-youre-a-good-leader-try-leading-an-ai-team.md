# HBS AI Institute — Think you're a good leader? Try leading an AI team

## TL;DR

A ~970-word research summary by the [[HBS AI Institute]]'s content team, published 3 September 2026, of [[2025-08-05-weidmann-xu-deming-measuring-leadership-skills-ai-agents|Weidmann, Xu & Deming (2025)]]. Deming is a 2026 associate of the institute. It restates the paper in three "key insights" and adds one of its own:

1. **One test, two kinds of teammates.** 249 leaders each led six human teams and six teams of GPT-4o agents on Hidden Profile puzzles.
2. **The leader, not the luck of the draw.** Leader identity explains more than half the variation in team performance; AI-test and human-test scores correlate 0.67 raw, 0.81 corrected; a good leader solves 53% of problems, a weak one 10%.
3. **Successful leaders ask, listen, and reassess.** *"If you assume the best leaders are the ones who talk the most, the data politely disagrees."* Questions, turn-taking and "we" predict success; warmth matters more with human followers.
4. **The institute's own addition: AI as a leadership practice environment.** *"Managers can rehearse questioning, delegation, information gathering, and decision-making with AI teams, further enhanced by employing a range of personas. Instead of treating leadership development as an occasional workshop, leaders and organizations can build short, repeatable simulations around familiar challenges."* It adds a caution: simulations suit *"the mechanics of coordination,"* but development must still address *"the trust, morale, and emotional judgment that shape real human teams."*

## How it relates to the paper

The figures are reported accurately. Two differences in emphasis:

- **Use case.** The paper argues for **measurement**: selecting leaders, evaluating training, cheaper research. The summary argues for **practice**: rehearsing leadership with AI teams. The paper does not test whether practising with AI agents improves leadership; that is the summary's extrapolation.
- **What is left out.** The summary omits the authors' main stated limitation, that the AI test has not been validated against leadership success in real organisations, and the cost comparison ($114 vs $23 per participant).

It also misspells co-author Yixian Xu as "Yixian Yu" in the author bios.
