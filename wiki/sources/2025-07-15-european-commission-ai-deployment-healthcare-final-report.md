# Study on the deployment of AI in healthcare — European Commission (PwC and Open Evidence), 2025

> Despite the promise and availability AI-based tools in the market, their deployment in clinical practice is slow.
>
> — the report's abstract (p. 2)

## TL;DR

A 241-page study for the Commission's health directorate on how far AI is deployed in EU health care, what blocks it, and what the EU could do. It combines a literature review, a market analysis of AI medical devices, five stakeholder surveys, interviews, workshops and four case studies.

1. **The study warns against its own kind of evidence.** *"Surveys may lead to overly optimistic estimations of AI deployment in healthcare since participants are usually more familiar with these technologies than the average healthcare professional"* (p. 41).
2. **Claimed use and evidenced use differ.** Of EU-based clinicians answering, **63% (29 of 46)** said they use AI tools in clinical practice; only **20 of the 46 (43%)** gave evidence of actual use of AI medical devices. Some named ChatGPT (p. 42).
3. **The hospital survey is small.** **35 hospital representatives** answered: 20 pilot an AI solution, 19 have bought and deployed a commercial one, 11 run an in-house one, 2 have adopted none (p. 42). Among adopters, *"only a minority track specific performance or efficiency metrics"* (p. 156).
4. **Deployment lags research and approvals.** Despite rising R&D and device approvals, *"there is a large disconnect between the amount of research and development on AI medical devices and their adoption in clinical practice"* (p. 40). The study's scenarios for deployment in clinical practice range from **5%** to **48%**, with 27% in between (p. 46).
5. **The 94% in the report is about radiology's potential.** **94%** of 32 hospital representatives name radiology as the area with the most transformative potential (Annex 4, p. 153). No figure in the report says 94% of providers use or plan AI.

## Method and definitions

- **Who.** Written by PwC EU Services EEIG and Open Evidence for DG SANTE, Unit C.1 Digital Health; study period January 2024 – May 2025; manuscript completed June 2025. The cover says the views are the authors', not the Commission's.
- **Surveys.** Five targeted surveys on EUSurvey (patients, HCPs, hospital representatives, AI developers, regulatory experts), launched 10 June 2024 with translations from 3 July 2024 (p. 10). The closing date is printed as *"25th September 2025"*, which must mean 2024. Stakeholders were picked for *"exposure to deployment of AI"* (p. 16).
- **"Deployment"** means AI/ML-enabled medical devices in clinical practice. The study measures it three ways: literature estimates (job postings, surveys), registers of approved and CE-marked devices, and its own surveys.
- **Scenarios** (p. 46) are assumptions, not measurements: the 5% baseline follows Goldfarb et al.'s US job-posting estimate; the 48% best case follows the share of radiologists in a European Society of Radiology survey who claimed to use AI.

## Scope and reliability

- **No representative hospital figure.** The hospital survey has 35 respondents, chosen for AI exposure; 32 answered the transformative-potential question. The authors call their own survey results potentially *"biased estimations"* (p. 42) and *"positively biased"* (p. 42).
- **Self-report is the problem the study names.** Respondents may count rule-based decision support or general tools such as ChatGPT as AI (pp. 41–42, 46).
- **A consultancy study for a regulator.** It reports stakeholder views on the AI Act, MDR, EHDS and the Product Liability Directive without a full regulatory assessment (p. 16).
- **Partly read.** The four case studies and the full monitoring framework were not read closely.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-sensing/digital-scouting`**: an EU-wide scan of where AI in health care stands, from research and device approvals through to use in clinical practice.
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`**: the organisational and social challenges it lists (lack of end-user involvement, no added-value assessment, no AI strategy, lack of trust, job-security fears) are barriers inside hospitals, alongside the external regulatory ones.
