# Collison — Will agents actually be able to buy things on the internet? (Browserbase Navigate, Oct 2026)

> Will Agents actually be able to buy things on the internet? Stripe Co-Founder John Collison thinks so.
>
> We hosted him as our keynote speaker at Navigate 2026 to talk about agentic commerce, computer use, the singularity, and their recent acquisitions.
>
> — channel description, Browserbase

## TL;DR

A ~29-minute keynote conversation on the **Browserbase** channel, published 5 October 2026, recorded at Browserbase's **Navigate 2026** conference in San Francisco. **John Collison**, co-founder of **Stripe**, is interviewed on stage by **Molly O'Shea**. Browserbase sells headless browsers for AI agents, and Collison says it is *"well on its way to becoming a huge Stripe company"*: host and guest each have a commercial interest in the thesis. The captions are auto-generated; quotes are cleaned for names only. There are no slides: the stills scan found only stage footage.

**1. Agents need computer use, "the ultimate backwards compatibility layer" (0:25–2:20).** Stripe processed *"$1.9 trillion in 2025, up 34%"* (the host's figure). Personal agents such as Instinct and Muse are *"real"*. *"Filling out a web form is not a value-add activity for anyone."* An agent that knows your shipping address and payment details, and can operate a computer, can *"renew my driver's license for me."* *"How long would you need to wait for … the DMV to add an MCP? … it doesn't matter because computer use solves that."*

**2. Agentic commerce rewires selling in two orders (2:20–4:45).** First order: *"people's agents are going to be doing a huge amount of the commerce for them."* Second order: AI product research *"often actually surfaces niche brands or businesses you haven't heard of … quite a good story for the long tail."* *"AI discovery is the new point of aggregation for commerce"*, so incumbent aggregators face questions and the top sellers will turn over. His analogy: Facebook and Instagram ad targeting produced a generation of native sellers (Wish, Teespring, Temu, Shein) that out-adopted big-box retailers with the same tools.

**3. Cybersecurity gets worse before equilibrium (4:45–6:52).** The new models *"have really advanced cyber capabilities"*; *"over the next 5 years, you're going to see more breaches than you did over the past 5 years"* because many organisations *"are going to be too slow in how they adopt the latest capabilities."* Startups are better placed, with *"smaller, more contained, more homogeneous tech stacks."*

**4. "Day one of the singularity" was 1 January 2026 (6:53–9:30).** A Stripe in-joke, set after the *"holy crap moment"* with coding models at the end of 2025. *"AI capabilities are helping with the rate of improvement in AI."* The economic signal he cites: *"We're seeing the highest rates of new firm creation that we've seen in recent memory on Stripe."* No figure is given.

**5. Developer ergonomics give way to AI ergonomics (9:35–11:40).** Stripe spent its history on the developer experience of its API. *"Relatively overnight, no one actually really integrates the Stripe API by hand anymore … they send their coding agent to do it."* What helps an agent differs: *"it's very easy for them to … integrate the wrong version of the API."* *"We are having to kind of totally change what we're good at internally at Stripe."* For every industry, *"everyone's chess boards just got a little bit of a jiggle."*

**6. The acquisitions, read through AI (11:40–15:20).** Bridge and Privy bring crypto-native culture. **Metronome** is usage-based billing, because *"because of AI, everyone has inference costs"*. **OpenRouter** bets that *"more than one model will be used inside a business"*, routed by workload or by the user's lifetime value. How much of Stripe's volume is AI: *"a lot"*, but hard to measure, since *"if you're not using a lot of AI in your business, you're probably not paying attention."* Build versus buy: buy when the muscle is new and the market moves fast enough that an internal build would be out of date on arrival.

**7. Inside Stripe: AI on internal data, built by its users (17:29–20:20).** He cites the [[jagged-frontier]]: models do well where there is training data and poorly elsewhere; the organisational version is that consumer apps lack business context. Stripe built an internal tool that gives AI access to internal data, *"which is quite complex because you need to have all the privacy guardrails … access control guardrails."* With it, *"all our sales teams … completely changed their workflows"*, and *"they are building their own application"*; someone on legal built an agent that ingests new regulation. *"People have been talking about personalized software for decades … finally the barrier to entry has gotten low enough."*

**8. Computer use as the next discrete hop (20:20–21:10).** Transformers, then LLMs, then RL; *"good computer use … will come to be viewed as one of these next hops."*

**9. The unasked question: bot detection when it is all bots (24:05–25:25).** *"Many sites do bot detection and bot prevention … but like it's increasingly all bots. And so, what is the new equivalent of … bot detection … where there is the good bots and then there are the bad bots?"* Try Instinct, Grok or Meta's Muse to see *"that new modality for how AI products will work"*, but *"I think we just don't know"* what the new equilibrium will be.

**10. Lessons from 17 years (25:30–28:17).** *"There's no shortcuts"*: no breakout moment, just growth every year; new products start as *"a toy"*; stick-with-it-ness (Nvidia as example); the long-term, *"infinite game"* with partners; and Stripe's first operating principle, *"users first"*.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **contextual/external-triggers** — agentic commerce and AI discovery change how every merchant is found; Collison frames it as the board being jiggled for every industry.
- **strategic-renewal/business-model** — Stripe's core competence (developer ergonomics) loses value as coding agents integrate the API, and the acquisitions (usage-based billing, model routing) follow the cost structure of AI products.

## Debates and supersession

- **Interested parties.** Browserbase sells computer-use infrastructure, and Stripe processes the payments of agentic commerce and counts Browserbase as a customer. The two claims carrying the talk (computer use is the next AI wave; agents will do much of the buying) come with no adoption figures.
- **Long tail or new aggregator?** Collison expects AI discovery to help well-reviewed niche sellers. The [[agentic-web]] page records the opposite worry from [[2026-05-14-price-dfi-retail-asia-reinventing-how-it-sells|DFI]] and Ognibeni: the agent operator captures the customer relationship. Both can hold if the aggregator changes and the long tail gains reach under it.
- **Computer use vs protocols.** Collison treats computer use as the way around waiting for MCP servers. The wiki's MCP-oriented sources assume the integration layer gets built; this is a bet on which arrives first, not a contradiction.
