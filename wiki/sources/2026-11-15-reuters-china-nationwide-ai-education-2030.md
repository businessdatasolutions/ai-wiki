# Reuters — China bets on nationwide AI education by 2030

> China is trying to implement AI education in classrooms nationwide by 2030, pushing ahead with integrating the technology into curricula even as parents and some researchers question its potential negative impact on childhood learning and development.
>
> *— Channel description, Reuters*

## TL;DR

A 3:34 [[Reuters]] video report, published 15 November 2026, filmed at the **Beijing New Talent Academy**, a $39,000-a-year private school whose own digital-innovation curriculum for more than 2,000 pupils includes flight simulation, robotics and programming. **Auto-generated transcript, and incomplete**: the pupil's and the headmaster's Mandarin remarks (about 1:04–1:26 and 1:34–2:01) are not in it, though the video carries English subtitles for both, read from the frames and given under [Visual canon](#visual-canon). No parent speaks; the parents' concerns named in the description reach the report only through the headmaster, who says in the subtitles that *"everyone worries"* at first. Corrected in quotation: "Shing Jang" → Xinjiang, "exasperate" → exacerbate. "Xiao Yong" is rendered **Yong Zhao**: his lower-third at 3:09 reads *Zhao Yong, Education professor, University of Kansas*, in Chinese name order; the pupil's and headmaster's names are garbled in the transcript; on-screen lower-thirds give them, and the headmaster's reads *Liu Wei, Headmaster, Beijing New Talent Academy*.

What it adds to the wiki:

1. **A national target with stages.** AI literacy is to be *"universal in schools by 2030,"* with children introduced to basic concepts by age 6 and older pupils expected to *"understand AI logic, identify misinformation, and apply the technology in advanced projects."*
2. **A spending figure from documents.** Reuters reviewed more than 100 government procurement documents and found authorities committed *"over $31 million in the past year to AI classroom equipment, training, and teaching platforms."*
3. **No measure of success yet.** China *"has yet to establish a widely adopted framework for measuring pupils' AI literacy,"* so schools experiment, making it *"difficult to determine what success looks like."*
4. **An equity objection.** Zhao expects integration to be *"an uphill battle,"* and *"AI is very likely to exacerbate the disparities, like kids in Shanghai, kids in Beijing will have a lot more resources than kids in Gansu or kids in Xinjiang."*

## The school, and one pupil's rule

The headmaster says only a small minority of pupils will become AI scientists or engineers; for the rest, the aim is AI as *"an everyday tool for learning and creating."* A 13-year-old the school calls *"an AI prodigy"* says he tries to solve coding problems himself before asking AI, and asks for hints *"rather than complete answers."* He *"relies on AI for help but does not depend on it."* The showcase is an elite private school, the well-resourced end of the disparity Zhao describes.

The report places the policy within *"Beijing's broader drive to dominate global AI development."* [[2026-09-18-reuters-on-assignment-inside-chinas-ai-race|Reuters' On Assignment]] describes Beijing's push as diffusion of AI *"throughout various different sectors of the economy and even things like education."* Whether children should learn AI before building the skills needed to check it is debated in [[ai-deskilling]], where [[2026-08-01-bbc-ai-decoded-why-isnt-ai-working-for-your-company|Stephanie Hare on BBC AI Decoded]] argues that children should build reading habits before they use AI.
