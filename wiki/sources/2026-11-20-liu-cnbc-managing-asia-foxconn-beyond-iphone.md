# Young Liu (CNBC, Managing Asia) — Foxconn's turn to AI infrastructure

> Hon Hai Technology Group, better known as Foxconn, is betting on AI infrastructure to drive its next phase of growth.
>
> Chairman Young Liu tells Christine Tan that AI infrastructure could account for more than 60% to 65% of the company's revenue within three to five years. He discusses Foxconn's partnership with Nvidia, growing demand for AI servers and data centers, and why he sees no signs of AI overcapacity.
>
> Liu also reflects on the challenges facing Foxconn's electric vehicle ambitions, its push into satellites and space-based computing, and his efforts to build a new legacy after succeeding founder Terry Gou.
>
> *— Channel description, CNBC International*

## TL;DR

A 15-minute episode of CNBC International's *Managing Asia*, published 20 November 2026. **[[Christine Tan]]** interviews **Young Liu**, chairman of Hon Hai Technology Group (Foxconn) since 2019, at the company's annual Hon Hai Tech Day in Taipei, the day before the doors opened. Parts are recorded walking the exhibition floor, parts seated, with Tan's narration between them.

What it adds to the wiki:

1. **A contract manufacturer's revenue shift, stated by its chairman.** Foxconn's cloud and networking business has overtaken consumer electronics; Liu expects AI infrastructure, *"not edge AI devices,"* to be *"more than 60 to 65%"* of revenue in three to five years.
2. **The AI-server supply side**, from the firm that assembles a large share of it: about 40% global share by Tan's figure, *"probably next year… up to like 45%,"* possibly 50%. NVIDIA is *"our biggest partner,"* not the biggest customer; systems ship to customers *"like Microsoft or Oracle, but with Nvidia's chip inside."*
3. **A supplier's answer to the overcapacity question.** *"I don't see over capacity."* He sorts demand into four buyers (model makers, cloud providers, governments, enterprises) and says only the first two are buying so far.
4. **Targets for the AI data centre as a product** (the "token factory"): a cut in power use on a 1 GW site and a build time of *"6 to 9 months"* instead of a year and a half to two years.
5. **Admitted mistakes and a culture change**: the EV target of 5% of the global market by 2025 was missed, *"the biggest one"*; the culture he inherited from founder Terry Gou had *"no planning,"* and he replaced it with annual plans tracked every week.

A CEO interview at the company's own showcase. Every figure is Liu's or Tan's, unsourced, and several are ambiguous in the captions. Six booth displays, hand-picked as stills from contact sheets, show what Foxconn exhibited and the interview does not discuss: an agentic AI platform, its FoxBrain model work, the parts of its NVIDIA racks, and its satellite programme (see [Visual canon](#visual-canon)).

## The token factory (0:49–2:19)

Tan calls Tech Day *"Foxconn's own consumer electronics show… focused on one company and its partners."* At a model of what Foxconn calls a token factory, Liu gives two targets. Power: *"With current AI data center, we're talking about the power consumption, it's about 1 gigawatt. Our goal is to reduce it down to 10% about 100 megawatt"* (1:35). The sentence can be read as a 90% cut or as a saving of 10%, about 100 MW; the captions do not settle which. The means: *"We do the racks, the server parts by ourselves. We save power and the cooling here and there."* Build time: *"currently it takes about a year and a half to two years to build a data center like this. It's way too long,"* and he hopes for *"6 to 9 months"* (2:13). The model itself (still 2) shows containerised data-centre modules, an 800 V DC power block with a battery container, and an on-site substation.

## NVIDIA: partner, not customer (2:19–3:23)

Asked whether [[NVIDIA]] is the biggest customer: *"They are not our biggest customers. They're our biggest partner. We ship most systems directly to our customers like Microsoft or Oracle, but with Nvidia's chip inside of it. Nvidia is probably one of the largest supplier for us."* The partnership is also how Foxconn reads the road ahead: *"By working with Nvidia, we know what's coming… every year Nvidia will come out with a new solution… We are one of the early adopter of the technologies so that we can be the newest technology provider to our customers"* (3:00). This is the `digital-scouting` cell of sensing: scanning for technology trends through a partner's roadmap. It is also `navigating-innovation-ecosystems`: Foxconn positions itself inside NVIDIA's ecosystem as the integrator between the chip supplier and the cloud buyers ([[Microsoft]], Oracle).

[[2026-11-09-vahdat-lohmeyer-google-cloud-future-of-ai-infrastructure|Google Cloud's infrastructure keynote (Nov 2026)]] covers the buyer's side of the same hardware generation: Google offers instances on NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform next to its own TPUs. Foxconn's booth (still 3) shows Vera Rubin NVL72 racks, trays and cooling parts.

## The 3+3+3 strategy and the turn to AI (3:23–4:51)

Tan's narration: since taking over in 2019, Liu *"has looked for new growth drivers while keeping Foxconn's mobile phone business thriving."* His **3+3+3 strategy** pairs three industries (electric vehicles, digital health, robotics) with three core technologies (AI, semiconductors, next-generation communications), which feed three platforms (manufacturing, EV development and production, digital services for cities). This is `balancing-digital-portfolios`: new businesses scaled next to a core that still pays, at a chosen speed ("we're not in a hurry" on EVs, below).

*"Just recently, your cloud and networking business has overtaken your consumer electronics business."* Liu: *"I think Foxconn can be considered an AI-driven company and I think all the companies in the ICT industry will be AI-driven in the future eventually including the mobile phone industries."* Smartphones will not be made obsolete; they *"evolve together with the AI industry."* Three to five years out, AI infrastructure *"will be more than 60 to 65%"* of revenue (4:58).

## Market share and margins (5:05–6:49)

Tan: *"currently you claim to have about a 40% global market share"* in AI servers. Liu: *"I think we can grow up to 50%… probably next year our 40% market share will increase up to like 45%."*

On margins, Tan puts operating margin *"below 4%."* Liu's route to a higher gross margin is vertical integration: *"we had to do more components… We do components about 1.5 trillion NT dollar a year,"* which is *"the reason why… our gross profit margin is about 6%."* Asked whether that could take operating margin to 5%: *"I wouldn't say 5%. I would say to increase one more percent is very possible. This year we're going to do more than 10 trillion NT dollars. 1% is about 100 billion NT dollars."*

## No overcapacity, and four kinds of buyer (6:49–8:21)

Asked whether he sees evidence of AI overspending and overcapacity: *"I don't see over capacity. We found that that demand can be categorized into four categories. The first categories is from the model maker. The second category is from the CSPs. The third one is from the governments. The fourth one from the enterprises. What you have seen the demand so far is only for the first two. The third and fourth one they all waiting or learning how to use AI in their government or in their companies"* (7:11–7:47).

He claims an early-warning position, a second `digital-scouting` mechanism: *"because we do vertical integration. If there's anything is about to happen, we will sense it much quickly. And also because we cover 40, 50% of the industries, not only one customer, we know many other customers their situation,"* which lets Foxconn judge *"whether this is a global phenomena or just single company phenomena."*

[[2026-08-18-covello-gs-new-models-ai-investment-landscape|Jim Covello (Goldman Sachs, Aug 2026)]] makes a related point from an investor's seat: *"enterprises collectively are not making or saving money on their AI implementations,"* and *"all of the economic value is accrued to the semiconductor companies,"* which he calls unsustainable unless enterprise returns arrive. Both describe demand that so far comes from the builders of models and clouds; Liu reads the missing enterprise and government demand as demand still to come, Covello as the condition the build-out depends on. See [[enterprise-ai-adoption]].

## Electric vehicles: the 5% miss (8:21–10:05)

Three EV designs debuted at this year's event; Liu showed Tan the Model A+, designed with the Japanese market in mind. He had targeted 5% of the global EV market by 2025, and Foxconn fell short. On the MIH open EV platform, his idea: *"The challenge is not on technology, it's the industry… the car industry, they have a lot of legacy experiences. It's difficult to convince them to use a new way to design their cars."* Without MIH, *"it's difficult for the existing old players to adopt this new ideas, but we're not in a hurry… when the time comes, it will come."* MIH, as an open platform for other carmakers, is an ecosystem move (`navigating-innovation-ecosystems`). EV outsourcing margins are *"not bad… somewhere around 20"* (9:55); the unit and the kind of margin are not stated.

## Satellites and space-based computing (10:05–11:19)

Foxconn has launched four satellites. *"With these four satellites, we're trying to understand how the platform works. The purpose of that is for us to build the satellite platforms so that the payload companies they can use our satellites to carry their payloads such as data center components."* The space industry is *"low volume… we're from the IT industry. Our speed is much much faster,"* and *"there are number of space companies"* that asked Foxconn to speed up their production. Tan asks how close Foxconn is to manufacturing satellites for operators like SpaceX; Liu does not answer that directly. The exhibit (still 6) names the programme: PEARL CubeSat, two generations.

## Succeeding Terry Gou, and changing the culture (11:19–14:17)

Founder Terry Gou ran the company for 45 years before Liu succeeded him as chairman and CEO in 2019. *"When I took over, I told myself, I'm not going to be like him. I have to do something on my own."* First, expansion: revenue *"doubled from 5.3 trillion NT dollars to be more than 10 trillion this year."* Second, *"to make the management more systematic, including from how you define the future industry you want to get into, that kind of methodology."*

On changing a culture more than four decades old, which is `organizational-culture` renewal: *"You had to show by example. The example is not just by doing it. You have to first show some result… In the past there was no planning and they just tried their best. But I changed that. We had to do a annual plan and then we do it every week. We try to forecast whether we can make it or not. By forecasting it give us more time to react. So after a couple of years of meeting the goals then other leaders they look at this they say oh that's a good way."*

His biggest mistake: *"the EV, the 5% by 2025… with the new industry, we're not aware of all the existing industry players… they're so difficult to change and that was not expected. That was a mistake."*

## From manufacturer to platform (14:17–15:00)

*"I want Foxconn not just to make 6%. I want to transform Foxconn from a design and manufacturing company to a platform solution provider. Not just hardware but also the software."* This is `strategic-renewal/business-model` as a stated goal: from contract design and manufacturing toward platforms that include software, with AI infrastructure as the largest revenue line. The interview gives no figures for the software side. See [[dynamic-capabilities]] for the corpus's other accounts of incumbents renewing their business model.

## Source quality

A CEO interview recorded at the company's own showcase, the day before it opened, with b-roll of exhibits the company chose. Every figure is Liu's or Tan's and none is sourced on screen; the AI-server share is introduced by Tan as what Foxconn *"claim[s]"*. Figures the transcript cannot settle:

- **Power**: *"reduce it down to 10% about 100 megawatt"* on a 1 GW site may mean a cut to 10% or a 10% saving.
- **EV margin**: *"somewhere around 20"*, without a unit or margin type.
- **Margins**: Tan's *"below 4%"* is operating margin; Liu's *"about 6%"* is gross margin, so the two are not in conflict. His *"one more percent"* does not say which margin.
- **Share**: *"we cover 40, 50% of the industries"* reads as market share in AI servers, not a share of industries.
