# McKinsey — The state of AI in 2025: Agents, innovation, and transformation

## TL;DR

The November 2025 edition of the [[McKinsey & Company]] Global Survey on the state of AI, written by QuantumBlack, AI by McKinsey. An online survey of **1,993 participants in 105 nations**, fielded **25 June to 29 July 2025** (p. 29).

1. **Use is broad.** **88%** say their organisation regularly uses AI in at least one business function, against 78% a year earlier; **79%** for gen AI, against 71% (p. 4). The 2025 question asks about *regular* use; 2020–2024 asked whether the organisation had *adopted* AI (Exhibit 1 footnote, p. 4).
2. **Depth is thin.** Among organisations that use AI, 32% are experimenting, 30% piloting, 31% scaling and **7% "fully scaled"** (Exhibit 1, p. 4). The fully scaled share runs from 3% to 10% by company revenue, highest at $5 billion or more (Exhibit 5, p. 10).
3. **Agents are early.** **23%** are scaling an agentic AI system somewhere in the enterprise and 39% are experimenting (p. 5). In no single function do more than 10% report scaling agents (Exhibit 2, p. 5).
4. **Enterprise value is rare.** **39%** attribute any EBIT impact to AI, and most of those say it is under 5% of EBIT (p. 11).
5. **"AI high performers" are about 6%.** McKinsey's definition: respondents who attribute 5% or more of EBIT to AI *and* report "significant" value (p. 14). They redesign workflows 2.8 times as often (55% vs 20%, p. 16) and set growth or innovation goals far more often (p. 15).

## Method and definitions

- **Sample.** 1,993 online respondents "at all levels of the organization" in 105 nations; 38% work for organisations with more than $1 billion in revenue. Data are weighted by each nation's share of global GDP (p. 29). Respondents report on their own organisation; one respondent is not one firm.
- **Fieldwork.** 25 June to 29 July 2025 (p. 29).
- **Use.** "Regular use of AI in at least 1 function", out of 11 functions (pp. 4, 8). The wording has changed four times since 2017 (Exhibit 1 footnote, p. 4).
- **Phases.** Experimenting = "any use or early testing"; piloting = "implementing AI for a first use case"; scaling = "growing the deployment/adoption of AI across organization"; fully scaled = "AI has been fully deployed and integrated across organization" (Exhibit 1, p. 4).
- **AI agents.** "Systems based on foundation models capable of acting in the real world, planning and executing multiple steps in a workflow" (p. 5).
- **AI high performers.** Body text: EBIT impact "of 5 percent or more" plus "significant" value, "about 6 percent of respondents" (p. 14). Every exhibit footnote says "more than 5%" instead (pp. 14–21). The unweighted count is 109 of 1,993 (5.5%); the 6% is presumably GDP-weighted.
- **What drives high performance.** A relative-weights analysis of 31 variables (footnote, p. 16). Workflow redesign and human-in-the-loop validation rank among the strongest factors (pp. 16, 19). McKinsey says the practices match its *Rewired* framework of six dimensions (p. 19).
- **Sponsor.** McKinsey's own survey and analysis; no outside sponsor named.

## Scope and reliability

- **Self-report by individuals.** Everything is a respondent's view of their organisation, including EBIT attribution. Nothing is audited.
- **Definition change in the headline.** The 78% → 88% rise compares "adopted AI" (2024) with "regular use" (2025). The report draws the line without comment; the change is visible only in the Exhibit 1 footnote (p. 4).
- **"High performer" is a value threshold, not a maturity stage.** It counts respondents who claim ≥5% of EBIT from AI. It is not the same instrument as the 1% "mature" figure the wiki took from the [[2026-04-28-ai-index-report-2025|AI Index 2025]], nor as the "fully scaled" phase above.
- **Inconsistent threshold wording** ("5 percent or more" vs "more than 5%") and **two exhibits print n = 1,933** instead of 1,993 (pp. 5, 21). Small, but they matter if the 6% is compared across editions.
- **Consultancy instrument.** The practices tied to high performance are mapped to McKinsey's own *Rewired* framework, which the firm uses in client work (p. 19). The analysis is correlational and cannot show which way cause runs.
- **Later edition.** McKinsey published a 2026 edition in August 2026. It could not be reached for this ingest.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-scouting`**: an annual scan of how far organisations have taken AI and agents, by function, industry and company size.
- **`improving-digital-maturity`**: the experimenting → piloting → scaling → fully scaled phases measure maturity directly, and Exhibit 18 tracks hiring of AI roles (data engineers, ML engineers), more so in large firms (p. 25).
- **`balancing-digital-portfolios`**: the report is about scaling up beyond pilots, and about how much of the digital budget goes to AI (35% of high performers spend more than a fifth of it, p. 21).
- **`internal-enablers`**: senior-leader ownership (strongly agree: 48% of high performers vs 16% of others, p. 18), agile product delivery and defined human-in-the-loop processes are the practices most tied to value.
