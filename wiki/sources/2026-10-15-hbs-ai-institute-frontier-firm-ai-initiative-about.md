# HBS AI Institute — About the Frontier Firm AI Initiative

## TL;DR

The institutional "About" page of the **Frontier Firm AI Initiative**, run by the [[HBS AI Institute]] at Harvard Business School in collaboration with [[Microsoft]]. Undated; captured 15 October 2026. Two parts:

1. **What a Frontier Firm is.** *"A new class of organization that puts AI at the heart of its business strategy to reinvent how it operates, innovates, and augments human capacity at scale"*, from *"a one-person startup with a multimillion-dollar annual run rate to a large enterprise."* Member companies run experiments with Harvard and Microsoft researchers on *"AI-first work patterns and functions, starting with Sales, IT, and Finance,"* and attend C-suite workshops. Frontier Firms *"choose to participate in rigorous research instead of waiting for the playbook to be written by someone else."*
2. **Early insights** from an upcoming case by Iavor Bojinov, [[Raffaella Sadun]] and Shunyuan Zhang, in four findings below.

## Key claims: the early insights

### Adoption needs structured experimentation, and employees will not self-discover

*"Unlike standard technology, AI has a broader scope and greater depth of collaboration/automation, requiring a first phase of structured, frontline experimentation to discover new value. Do not assume that employees would self-discover uses without specific guidance."* Experimentation is followed by adaptation, then scaling.

### Four managerial problems in running the experiments

1. **What to experiment on:** *"wide enough for discovery, narrow enough for scaling."*
2. **Whom to select:** people *"who can tolerate uncertainty and short-term unproductiveness."*
3. **How to codify and scale what is learned.**
4. **How to structure incentives** for full-scale adoption.

### The Skill–Will 2×2

Employees are placed by **skill** (digital and AI literacy) and **will** (motivation to adopt):

| | High will | Low will |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High skill** | **Evangelists** — natural early adopters who pioneer new uses | **Skeptics** — resist; need reframing or incentives. *"Their conversion can have outsized influence due to their credibility."* |
| **Low skill** | **Learners** — motivated but need structured support (training, shared prompts); *"crucial spreaders of established use cases"* | **Resisters** — avoid experiments; poor experiment subjects, but must be brought along once the technology is settled |

### Agents make it harder

AI agents *"exacerbate adoption challenges."* They need more substantive onboarding, which means codifying tacit knowledge and adds a need for **"agent management skills"**. They also create a sharper *"winners and losers"* dynamic, *"potentially increasing the performance gap and resistance among employees."*

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-seizing/rapid-prototyping`** — the first adoption phase is structured frontline experimentation, scoped *"wide enough for discovery, narrow enough for scaling,"* with people chosen for tolerance of short-term unproductiveness.
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`** — the Skill–Will matrix is a way of identifying workforce digital maturity, with structured support for Learners and C-suite programmes as the levers.
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`** — Skeptics and Resisters are the change resistance the cell names, and agents widen the gap between winners and losers, which raises resistance further.
