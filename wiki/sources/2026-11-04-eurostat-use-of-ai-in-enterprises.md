# Use of artificial intelligence in enterprises (Eurostat, Statistics Explained)

## TL;DR

Eurostat's standing article on AI use by EU firms, from the EU survey on ICT usage and e-commerce in enterprises. This snapshot carries the 2025 data. The page was last edited on 4 November 2026; its data were extracted in December 2025, and the next update is planned for 15 December 2026.

1. **One in five EU firms uses AI.** In 2025, 19.95% of EU enterprises with 10 or more employees and self-employed persons used at least one of eight AI technologies, up 6.47 points on 2024 (13.48%).
2. **Size drives use.** 17% of small, 30.36% of medium and 55.03% of large enterprises used AI.
3. **Countries range eightfold.** From 5.21% in Romania to 42.03% in Denmark. The Netherlands is at 33.21% (2024: 23.06%), and Eurostat marks the Dutch 2025 figure as a break in the time series.
4. **No technology dominates.** Text mining leads at 11.75%, then generating pictures, video or sound (9.55%) and generating text, speech or code (8.76%).
5. **Firms use AI mostly for marketing and administration.** Among AI users, 34.70% use it for marketing or sales and 31.05% for business administration or management.
6. **Expertise is the main barrier.** Among firms that considered AI but did not use it, 70.89% cite lack of expertise, 52.52% unclear legal consequences and 48.83% data-protection concerns. Only 20.68% say AI is not useful to them.

**By sector, 2025**: information and communication 62.52% · professional, scientific and technical 40.43% · real estate 24.82% · manufacturing 17.27% · construction 10.79%.

## Method and definitions

- **Survey.** National statistical authorities ran the survey in the first months of 2025. 157,000 of the EU's 1.53 million enterprises were surveyed; 83% of the population is small, 14% medium and 3% large.
- **Unit and coverage.** The enterprise, with at least 10 employees and self-employed persons. Covered: manufacturing, energy and water, construction, trade, transport, accommodation and food, information and communication, real estate, professional and scientific activities, administrative and support services, and computer repair. Financial services, health care, education and public administration are outside the scope.
- **What counts as use.** At least one of eight listed technologies. [[2026-02-10-cbs-ict-gebruik-bij-bedrijven-2025|CBS]] notes that the eighth, generating pictures, video or sound, was added in 2025. The page itself does not say so.
- **Aggregation.** Unreliable national data are included in EU aggregates.

## Scope and reliability

- **Official statistics** with a common EU questionnaire and a large sample, unlike the vendor and consultancy surveys most adoption figures in the corpus come from.
- **Breadth only.** A firm counts if it uses any one technology. Nothing is measured about depth, scale within the firm, or value.
- **Firm-weighted.** Every firm counts once, and 83% of the population is small, so the EU total is close to the small-firm rate.
- **No hospitals.** Health care is outside the survey's scope, so this page gives no figure for hospitals.
- **Breaks and definition changes.** The Netherlands' 2025 figure is flagged as a break in series without explanation on the page. CBS's StatLine notes a new weighting method from 2025. The added eighth technology affects every country.
- **A living page with loose ends.** The "Data sources" text says the data are *"based on the results of the 2024 EU survey"* while every figure is labelled 2025, and the highlight box links a file titled for 2023 and 2024. The figures in this snapshot match the page's own source-data spreadsheet.
