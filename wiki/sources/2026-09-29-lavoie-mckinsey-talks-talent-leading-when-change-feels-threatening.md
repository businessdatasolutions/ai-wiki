# Lavoie — Leading When Change Feels Threatening (McKinsey Talks Talent, Sep 2026)

> Why do people resist change? And what can leaders do when fear is holding a team back?
>
> Leaders often focus on resistance to change, but resistance isn't the full story. Fear of loss is a huge part of transformation, too, when roles, routines, and relationships are redefined.
>
> On this episode of _McKinsey Talks Talent,_ McKinsey Partner Johanne Lavoie shares her latest research on how leaders can support their teams through times of strain with Senior Partner Brooke Weddle, Partner Bryan Hancock, and Global Editorial Director Lucia Rahilly. They discuss why leaders who understand fear, rather than punish it, end up with more resilient teams that make sharper decisions and exhibit stronger performance under pressure.

This is a **change-leadership** episode, not an AI episode. AI enters at three points (see *Where AI comes in*). The page is in the wiki because those points frame AI-driven restructuring as a problem of fear, trust and constant change, and because of the leader competences Lavoie says AI will not supply. **No figures are given.** The description's claim of stronger performance under pressure rests on Lavoie's client work, two interviews with former Navy SEALs, and the unnamed article.

**1. Under threat, people fall into reactive patterns (1:31–3:16).** In a high-stakes leadership meeting *"you can feel it. The tension rises because there's no easy answer."* Some people get aggressive, some withdraw *"on a spreadsheet… the safety of the intellect and the number,"* and some seek harmony. The financial problem on the table triggers a psychological one: *"not a threat to your physical survival but… a threat to my status, my credibility, my belonging, my competence."* The prescription: *"if I could meet people in the fear as opposed to judging the behavior, then we're going to have the right conversation."*

**2. People don't resist change; they react to fear of loss (3:31–6:07).** A leader who says change fails *"because people are resisting it"* is *"putting the accountability on them."* She suggests two moves:
- say first *"what will not change and how will the change reinforce what we value"*;
- name the risks and ask *"what do we need to be willing to let go?"*

Her example is a hospital executive, a nurse by training, who holds a monthly **"funeral meeting"** about projects being stopped or deprioritised: what was learned, what was liked, what is carried forward. *"Fear needs to be processed. If you don't create a space for it, it will go into resistance."*

**3. Restructuring is actual loss, not only anticipated loss (7:07–10:43).** Hancock raises it: restructurings and layoffs *"are like everywhere."* Lavoie: how the leavers are treated matters, and so does how the people who stay are treated. They need moments of *"simply listening… without trying to fix them"*, because *"strong emotions… fear, loss, grief heal in connection."*

**4. Change is constant, so strain accumulates (12:04–15:33).** *"We used to have change, then you could actually have a period and then another change… You can't manage the change today. The change is constant."* Without recovery between changes, strain piles up: *"Let's put a well-being activity on top of this. That's not going to fix it."* Her remedies:
- a team **"nest"** where people can recharge while being held to high accountability;
- team rituals such as **pre-mortems and post-mortems**;
- teaching people to regulate their stress;
- leaders as **storytellers, pattern recognisers and system designers**.

**5. Safety and accountability are not a trade-off (20:56–22:11).** Asked whether nests conflict with holding people accountable: *"It's a false tension… the more you turn on the heat, the more you need psychological safety."* A CEO she quotes: *"If you have really high aspiration, you need a depth of foundation of trust"* to hold it up.

**6. Three axes for a team (23:07–26:21).** *"I'm not a big fan of team building for the sake of team building… You build the team as you work together."*
1. A **shared understanding of current reality**, using a team diagnostic: top three strengths, three things holding the team back.
2. A **shared narrative**: teams aligned on numbers often are *"not aligned on how you get there."*
3. **Trust-building moves in the moment**: *"If there's tension in a room, name it. Cuz when you name it, you can tame it."*

**7. The CEO's role (26:21–30:33).** From *"driving the results to creating the environment that will deliver the results"*. The CEO pays attention to what in the system is producing stress and resistance, and is *"the master storyteller."* Weddle recounts a CEO who shared his change narrative with his top leaders and asked for feedback. Lavoie calls this **"disciplined humility"**; in her example a CEO came back the next day to apologise publicly for a badly answered question. *"What differentiates a decision from a choice is the fear… We'll talk about data and we want the data to solve the answer. It's not going to solve the answer."*

**8. Navy SEALs (30:33–32:22).** From two former SEALs interviewed for the research: performance depends on investment in *"mental, physical, emotional, spiritual health and support, and that includes their families."* On high-stakes missions: *"you don't communicate… my job is to enroll them in the mission."*

## Where AI comes in

Three passages, all framing AI as the reason change no longer arrives in episodes:

- **Weddle's question (10:47–11:34):** restructuring *"is actually more dynamic than it's ever been because of AI,"* and organisations need *"a change narrative that balances this notion of abundance and growth"* with *"the real fear that people have about this moment and what AI could potentially mean from the perspective of their job and their livelihood and their value. So how does this notion of listening… really scale?"* Lavoie's answer is section 4 above.
- **The competence claim (15:11–15:23):** *"Leaders need more emotional intelligence and pattern recognition and systems thinking and storytelling in a world of AI, because AI is not going to do it for you."* She repeats it at 29:14: the world *"will trigger human emotion. AI is not going to solve that for us."*
- **The closing analogy (31:58):** *"AI has nonlinear outcome right now. How do we enroll people? How do we create that psychological safety so that we can turn on the heat without having a brittle environment that will break?"*

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`strategic-renewal/organizational-culture`.** The whole episode is about the cultural conditions for change: psychological safety, trust, rituals that process loss, and leaders who regulate their own reactions. It treats these as performance infrastructure, not as well-being add-ons.
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`.** Fear of loss is the barrier named. Lavoie's point is that "resistance to change" mislabels it, and that the label puts accountability on the people being changed.
- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`.** Restructurings and layoffs, made *"more dynamic than ever because of AI"*, are the setting for sections 3 and 4. The proposed structures are team rituals, nests, and the CEO as designer of the system that produces results.
