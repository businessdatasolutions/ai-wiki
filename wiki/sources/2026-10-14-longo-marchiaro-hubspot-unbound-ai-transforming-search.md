# Longo & Marchiaro (HubSpot UNBOUND 2026) — how AI is transforming search

> AI is changing how people discover, evaluate, and engage with businesses. Paul Longo, General Manager of AI in Ads at Microsoft Advertising, and Julian Marchiaro, Senior Director of Technology Strategy at Merkle, break down how marketers can reach high-value audiences and capture emerging demand, how the Microsoft Advertising and HubSpot integration connects ad data with CRM insights, and what shifting customer expectations mean for media operations and growth strategy.

## TL;DR

A 38-minute session from HubSpot's UNBOUND 2026 conference on the **HubSpot Live** channel, published 14 October 2026. Two parts: a ~20-minute keynote by **Paul Longo**, who runs AI advertising at [[Microsoft]] Advertising, and a ~17-minute fireside with **Julian Marchiaro** of the agency Merkle. It is a vendor keynote that ends in a product launch: the first integration between [[HubSpot]] and Microsoft Advertising. What it adds to the wiki:

1. **Three eras of the web, running at once.** Longo's frame, built on his own tennis shopping: buying grip tape he already knows (search — *"help me find it"*, the **human web**); choosing tennis shoes by asking Copilot (*"help me choose"*, the **LLM web**); having Copilot plan a French Open trip (*"do it for me"*, the **agentic web**). *"We're not moving from one to the other. All three are happening at the same time,"* and brands must win in all three.
2. **Consumer-readiness numbers.** From a Microsoft Advertising survey of 1,000 US adults (2026, "Race to Zero UI"): **67%** like or love automation for simple tasks, **54%** are comfortable with AI chatbots or conversational search, **60%** are *"ready for AI agents to replace traditional websites."* The same survey splits consumers 30 / 40 / 30 by how much support they want.
3. **Agent traffic.** *"AI traffic grows 8x faster than human traffic,"* and *"total agentic browser traffic"* grew almost 8,000 year over year (Human Security's 2026 benchmarks, per the slide). Customer journeys that include Copilot are **53% more likely** to end in a purchase within 30 minutes.
4. **Measure AI visibility.** Microsoft Clarity, a free site-analytics tool with about three million installs, now reports which AI conversations a brand appears in, its share of those conversations, and recommendations to improve; *"you can't optimize what you can't measure."*
5. **Marchiaro: use the freed time on purpose.** *"Organizations, particularly the large ones, are good at carrying inefficiencies. The automation creates slack… How is that slack being used?"* Map how you work before automating, define an innovation ambition, give someone the full-time job, and stop waiting for the best model.

## Key claims

### The human web is still most of the money

Longo does not say search is dying. Bing has just passed **one billion monthly active users**, *"the majority of clicks are still coming through the human web"* and so is most advertising revenue. What changes is that search is *"becoming more conversational,"* so fixed keyword lists *"can't cut it anymore."* Microsoft's answers are Performance Max (one campaign across Bing, Edge, Outlook, MSN, Xbox and LinkedIn surfaces) and AI Max, which matches ads to conversational queries; the slide claims AI Max *"delivers 8% increase in conversions on Search."* Longo adds a targeting argument unrelated to AI: 49% of US personal spending now comes from the top 10% of earners, up from 43% in 2020, and Microsoft's audience skews towards them.

### The LLM and agentic web: be in the conversation

*"Consumers are having conversations about brand decisioning in LLMs. Those LLMs are going to your websites to pull your website content into that conversation."* Longo's test for the audience: *"What's the most valuable conversation that you want to make sure that you're showing up in? And how confident are you that your brand will show up?"* The slide states it more sharply: *"It reasons. It decides. It buys. But does it pick your brand?"*

His prescriptions, paid and organic:

- **Paid** — the same Performance Max and AI Max campaigns, which can appear on new AI surfaces and against conversational queries.
- **Measurement** — Microsoft Clarity's AI-visibility view: conversations a brand appears in, share of conversation, and improvement recommendations. Free.
- **Feeds** — *"don't sleep on your feeds"*: product feeds in Microsoft Merchant Center are *"a core component of ensuring you show up in LLMs,"* with UCP enabled so products are represented correctly in LLM environments. (UCP is not expanded on stage.)
- **On-site agents** — a "brand agent" powered by Clarity that *"enables agentic commerce on your site,"* left for the booth.

### The CRM closes the loop

The launch: a HubSpot–Microsoft Advertising integration that turns HubSpot contacts into audiences across the Microsoft network, retargets them, and reports *"every contact, deal, and dollar right in HubSpot."* Longo says it was the integration HubSpot customers asked for most.

### Marchiaro: innovation by design, context over data, slack used deliberately

The fireside moves from products to operating questions:

- **Innovating by accident.** *"In many organizations we're still innovating by accident, not by design."* His question to the audience: is your team structured for innovation, or is it *"happening in pockets"*?
- **Modern CRM is context, not data.** *"Data in itself is not a moat anymore. It's not a differentiator. It's the context that you can bring with it in service of decisioning,"* and the next frontier is making that decisioning real time: a nurture that reacts to the webinar a lead just attended or the sales rep they ghosted, not only to their pipeline stage. He calls the shift one from customer centricity to *"human centricity."*
- **Media.** Activating first-party data in media (suppressions, look-alikes, precision targeting) is *"a proven path"*; AI recommendations on moving budget between campaigns and placements are early.
- **Slack.** AI is freeing time for *"higher level strategic work,"* such as weighing the options an AI recommends against one's own understanding of the business, or managing publisher relationships. But *"organizations, particularly the large ones, are good at carrying inefficiencies. The automation creates slack… How is that slack being used? How are the efficiencies transforming into growth and results for the business? And the question is, are you being intentional?"*
- **What to do first.** Map how you work today, or *"how can you go identify the next bottleneck to solve for"*; and *"it's not just about … using AI as glue to stitch together broken processes, but it's rethinking your process."* Define the innovation ambition. Free up a team, because such initiatives are often done *"on the side of their desk"* when *"that's a full-time job."*
- **Model parity.** Citing a recent Stanford report, he says models are reaching parity on productivity, so the choice matters little early on: *"just go deep in the stack that you have available. Use the integrations you already have access to."*
