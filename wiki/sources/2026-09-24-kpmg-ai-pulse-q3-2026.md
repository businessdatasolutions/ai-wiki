# KPMG AI Quarterly Pulse Survey, Q3 2026

## TL;DR

KPMG US's quarterly survey of large-company leaders, released 24 September 2026. 314 US C-suite and business leaders at organisations with $1bn+ revenue, surveyed 24 July to 25 August 2026.

1. **58% report measurable value.** *"Nearly 6 in 10 leaders (58%) report measurable business value from their AI initiatives"* (deck p. 2). Productivity is the most common kind (55%); financial outcomes 37%.
2. **Agents, by one count, 62%.** The release: *"62% of organizations report they are now building, deploying or developing AI agents, up from 53% last quarter."* The deck labels the same 62% "Deployment" and compares it with 42% in Q3 2025 (p. 3).
3. **Multi-agent work jumps.** Organisations actively developing or implementing multi-agent systems: 25%, against 6% in each of the two previous quarters (release; deck p. 3).
4. **Governance confidence jumps too.** 73% are confident in their governance and capabilities to manage AI risk at scale, up from 57% last quarter; 49% have defined high-risk cases where agents may not decide alone (deck p. 5).
5. **Cost discipline.** 74% include cost reviews in AI approvals (61% last quarter), 70% use monitoring dashboards, 43% have usage or token budgets (deck p. 2).

## Method and definitions

- **Sample:** 314 US-based C-suite and business leaders at organisations with annual revenue of $1bn or more; more than a third have revenue of $10bn or more. Fieldwork 24 July to 25 August 2026. Part of KPMG's Global AI Pulse.
- **"Measurable business value"** is the respondent's own judgement. No threshold, metric or question wording is given.
- **The sample changes each quarter.** Q2 2026 had 204 respondents (28 April to 25 May 2026); Q3 has 314.

**How the agent series is worded across quarters.** The Dutch report says KPMG changes its agent definitions between quarters. The releases show changes in wording; they do not document a change of question.

| Quarter | Headline | Wording |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Q1 2025 | 11% | "full deployment remains stagnant at 11%" |
| Q2 2025 | 33% | "deployed at least some agents" |
| Q3 2025 | 42% | "Deployment" (in the Q3 2026 deck) |
| Q1 2026 | 55% | "AI agent deployment" (in the Q2 2026 deck) |
| Q2 2026 | 53% | "AI agent deployment holds steady" |
| Q3 2026 | 62% | "building, deploying or developing AI agents" (release); "Deployment" (deck) |

The Q2 2026 deck (p. 6) shows the headline next to sub-items. Three of them (scaling agents across functions 29%, multi-agent systems 6%, orchestrating agents across workflows 18%) add up to the Q2 headline of 53%. The same three add up to 48% for Q1, against a stated 55%. How the headline is composed is not documented. The investment series also jumps: Q2 2026 reports a weighted average of $202M (Q1: $207M), Q3 reports $173M and compares it with Q3 2025, not with Q2.

## Scope and reliability

Self-report by senior leaders at large US companies, by a firm that sells AI advisory services. Quarterly samples of 200-300 change in size and are not described as a panel. The headline wording for agents widens over time, from "full deployment" to "at least some" to "building, deploying or developing". Read the agent figures as a series of separate snapshots, not as a clean trend. The value question has no threshold, which puts it among the broad measures.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: governance confidence, cost reviews, monitoring dashboards and token budgets are reported as signs of maturing AI operations.
- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: the move from piloting (55% to 25%) to deployment, and cost reviews in AI approval, are portfolio decisions.
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`**: data readiness and access is the top barrier to agent deployment (53%).
