# Grow Enterprise AI Maturity for Bottom-Line Impact (MIT CISR, August 2025)

## TL;DR

An [[MIT CISR]] research briefing by [[Stephanie Woerner]], [[Ina Sebastian|Ina M. Sebastian]], [[Peter Weill]] and Evgeny Káganer. It re-measures the four-stage enterprise AI maturity model that CISR introduced in December 2024, with a new and much smaller survey.

1. **New distribution, same scoring rule.** Stage 1 13%, Stage 2 23%, Stage 3 **46%**, Stage 4 **18%** in 2025, against 28 / 34 / 31 / **7%** in 2022 (figure, "Four Stages of Enterprise AI Maturity, 2022–2025").
2. **Different and much smaller sample.** The 2025 figures come from the *"MIT CISR 2025 Real-Time Business Survey (N=152)"*; the 2022 figures from the 2022 Future Ready Survey (N=721) (footnote 2; figure source note).
3. **Stage 4 performs best.** Stage 4 enterprises are **+13.9 pp** on growth and **+9.9 pp** on profit against their industry average; Stage 1 is −26.5 pp and −15.1 pp. The measures are from 2024 (figure source note).
4. **The headline claim** is that *"the greatest financial impact is achieved in progressing from stage 2 … to stage 3"*. Four challenges stand in the way: Strategy, Systems, Synchronization, Stewardship. Guardian Life and Italgas illustrate them.
5. **Not a page about 2026.** CISR's March 2026 follow-up, a session presentation called *"Scaling Enterprise AI Maturity for Bottom-Line Impact"*, is *"available only to logged-in members and attendees of the event"*. No public 2026 distribution exists.

## Method and definitions

- **Scoring rule (unchanged).** Respondents are grouped *"using a measure of Total AI Effectiveness, the equally weighted combination of three measures: effectiveness of AI to (i) improve operations, (ii) improve customer experience, and (iii) support and develop ecosystems."* On a 0–100% scale, Stage 1 = 0–49%, Stage 2 = 50–74%, Stage 3 = 75–99%, **Stage 4 = 100%**. The December 2024 briefing uses the same wording and the same cut-offs.
- **"Mature" here** means Stage 4: a self-rated Total AI Effectiveness score at the top of the scale. It is not a separate outcome measure. Growth and profit are reported per stage as percentage points above or below industry average.
- **Samples.** 2022: Future Ready Survey, fielded October 2022, N=721 (XXIV-12). 2025: Real-Time Business Survey, N=152. The briefing does not say who was invited, who answered, or when the 2025 survey ran. It says only that it ran *"during a period marked by the widespread adoption of generative AI and experimentation with agentic AI."*
- **Interviews.** Footnote 3 reports *"Nineteen interviews with executives in eleven enterprises from 2024 to 2025."* The figure's source note says *"twenty executives in nine enterprises."* The two counts do not match.
- **Sponsor.** CISR is funded by its member organisations. The briefing is written for them (*"published monthly to update the center's member organizations"*).

## Scope and reliability

- **Same rule, different population.** The cut-offs did not change, but the sample did: from 721 to 152 respondents, from a periodic survey to a "real-time" one. The briefing presents the two distributions side by side without discussing comparability. A share of 18% in a sample of 152 has a 95% margin of roughly ±6 points.
- **Self-report.** Stages come from respondents rating how effective their own AI is. Financial performance is benchmarked against industry average, but the briefing does not say whether it is self-reported or taken from accounts.
- **The headline claim and the figure.** The text says the 2→3 step brings the greatest financial impact. On the 2025 figure's own numbers, the steps in growth are 19.7 pp (1→2), 11.5 pp (2→3) and 9.2 pp (3→4); in profit 13.7, 2.2 and 9.1 pp. In the 2022 data the 2→3 step was the largest on both measures (14.8 and 10.9 pp). The briefing does not say how it measures "greatest financial impact".
- **Stage 3's lead shrank.** Stage 3's advantage fell from +11.3 / +8.7 pp (2022) to +4.7 / +0.8 pp (2024). Stage 3 is now the largest group (46%), so it sits close to the sample average.
- **Small print.** The figure labels the 2024 measures as "(2025)"; the interview counts disagree (above).

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: the whole briefing is a staged maturity model with a measured distribution; it is a direct reading of how far enterprises have come in improving digital maturity.
- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: the Stage 2→3 transition is about moving from a portfolio of pilots to scaled platforms. Guardian's three-phase value-tracking framework (hypothesis, test and business case, scale plan) is a portfolio filter.
- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`**: the Systems and Stewardship challenges are structural. Guardian reorganised technology around products and platforms with small cross-functional teams. Italgas redefined its CHRO role as a chief people, innovation and transformation officer and created an AI director and a group AI office.
