# Goel (BCG, The So What) — what AI is quietly taking from your workforce

> BCG's Sagar Goel explains how everyday AI use is already eroding the judgment organizations rely on—and what leaders can do about it. Sagar, leader of the Human Futures lab at the BCG Institute, shares what 70 C-suite leaders have told BCG about how "distributed de-skilling" shows up in daily work. Among the most vexing problems: AI outputs that go unchallenged, accountability that slips, and junior talent who build skills more slowly.
>
> You'll Learn: Why AI substitutes for thinking, not just supports it—and why that matters at scale. How to capture AI's productivity gains while protecting human judgment. How AI-free zones and simple team habits keep critical thinking sharp.

## TL;DR

A 16-minute episode of *The So What from BCG* on the [[Boston Consulting Group]] channel, published 3 November 2026. Host **Georgie Frost** interviews **Sagar Goel**, managing director and partner and global insights leader at the BCG Institute, where he leads the Human Futures Lab. It accompanies a co-authored article, *When Everyone Uses AI, Companies Risk Losing Critical Skills* (linked, not ingested). Five claims:

1. **Substitution, not support.** Calculators and GPS supported cognition; AI substitutes for it, and *"with agents, it's not individual tasks but whole workflows that get automated."*
2. **Distributed de-skilling.** BCG's name for skill loss spread across *"hundreds of thousands of people in an organization using AI pervasively."* In a study of 70 C-suite leaders and senior executives, more than half see it already, and 60% expect a material impact on their business within a few years.
3. **Three symptoms.** 90% of leaders report overreliance on AI outputs that are not stress-tested; more than 50% say AI takes the blame when an error is found; more than half see junior talent building skills too slowly, and more than a third see slower mentorship and coaching.
4. **The skills most at risk are problem framing and judgment.** Humans stay responsible for two things: framing the problem and setting the context, and stress-testing the output.
5. **Three counters:** an AI code of conduct with *"AI-free zones"*, workflow redesign that treats judgment, creativity and empathy as levers and not only efficiency, and skill building reinforced in daily work by managers.

## Key claims

### What de-skilling looks like

Goel's frame: *"human skills are not a fixed endowment. They are a renewable asset,"* which atrophy without *"constant feedback loops and constant repetition, much like we build muscles when going to a gym."* Junior work (research, drafting, analysis) is the work through which people learn, so taking it away slows the build. He does not conclude the junior route is closed: *"This only means a thoughtful intervention to actually build or invest in the leaders of tomorrow."* Firms that create value at scale treat AI as a growth tool: *"not about doing more with less but doing much more with the same."*

### Where AI should not go

He calls for *"AI-free zones"* and a *"clear AI code of conduct"* for high-risk work (regulatory, operational or strategic), for tasks that need ethical judgment, and for the origination of new ideas and brainstorming. Two cases, both unnamed: a large Indian bank holds an AI-free cross-functional session every Friday, alternating analytical, creative and brainstorming modes, and a large US telecoms company runs three-day hackathons with no AI to reinforce human-originated ideas. BCG itself aims to be *"the world's most AI-fluent workforce"* and runs tiered AI-fluency programmes, while a partner remains accountable for every recommendation to a client.

### Three signs on the desk

His personal rules for daily users, put as signs over a desk: *"Stop and think"* (frame the problem and your own first idea before prompting); *"I am in the driver's seat"* (own the stress-testing, for example by having the model argue from the customer's, the CFO's and an employee's point of view); and *"If in doubt, go back to the first."* For leaders: make stepping back the default, and keep *"safe spaces"* where teams discuss how they use AI and where they fell into *"analysis paralysis."*
