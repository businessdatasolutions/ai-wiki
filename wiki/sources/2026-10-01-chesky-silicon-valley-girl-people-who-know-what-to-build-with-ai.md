# Chesky — People who know what to build with AI will pull ahead (Silicon Valley Girl, Oct 2026)

> I spoke with Brian Chesky, Co-Founder & CEO of Airbnb, about how AI is changing the way people build companies, launch ideas, and stand out in a world where everyone has access to the same tools.
>
> — channel description, Silicon Valley Girl (first line; links, sponsor copy and chapter list omitted)

## TL;DR

A ~35-minute interview on **[[Silicon Valley Girl]]**, the YouTube channel of **Marina Mogilko**, published 1 October 2026. The guest is **Brian Chesky**, co-founder and CEO of **Airbnb**. It was recorded on the day of an Airbnb product launch (*"starting today"*), and the editors cut in Airbnb's own product visuals, eight of which are published below. The episode is sponsored by **[[HubSpot]]** (an ad read at 12:00–12:51). The captions are auto-generated; quotes are cleaned for names only.

**1. "AI-native" means every touch point gets smarter (1:52–4:50).** The launch: an **AI search** toggle that takes natural language and *"will also prefilter everything for you because Airbnb is very much about filters and controls"*; **AI-generated highlights** that summarise a listing *"personalized to you"*; asking the listing page a question instead of the host; an **AI comparison** tool; neighbourhood maps (Paris first, *"eventually … a thousand different cities"*). On the host side the app will *"feel more and more like an agent that can be your coach."*

**2. An agent is not a chatbot (2:49–5:40).** *"Chat bots aren't really great for shopping. Chat bots aren't really good for travel"*: they are text-based, hard to compare, not visual, not *"multiplayer"* (the average Airbnb booking is three people). *"Agent is an evolution of an application … A chatbot is a user interface. And I think because most agents are chatbots, we conflate the two."* And: *"the great thing about agents is they're interoperable. So one agent can talk to another agent."* See [[ai-agents]].

**3. A travel graph instead of a social network (5:59–8:35).** Social media has become *"performance platforms … broadcast platforms"*; Airbnb lets you connect with friends and see where they have stayed, because *"increasingly people want recommendations [from] their friends and their family rather than from … celebrities or writers."* The aim is to turn Airbnb *"from … just a marketplace of homes to a little more of a travel community."*

**4. Shipping 80% more features (8:36–10:16, 17:48–18:00).** *"We're shipping 80% more features than a year ago. Most of it's because of AI. … It used to take us a year to ship like a new service, now we can do it in weeks."* Services now include baby gear, laundry, ski equipment, groceries via Instacart. No method or baseline is given for the 80%.

**5. Services as a long tail (10:17–16:54).** A masseuse in Tulum or a photographer at the Eiffel Tower gets *"a global audience of travelers"*, as hosts once did for homes. Services are tested *"in one market, one city. If it works, then we bring it to other markets."* The opportunity is the long tail: most services are *"not big enough to have a global brand"* or an app.

**6. Token leaderboards were a phase; measure product velocity (16:55–17:52).** Early in the year *"CEOs were telling everyone, you got to use AI and they were measuring it by how many tokens you were using … There were like token leaderboards."* That *"might have been a good thing to do for a couple months"*, but *"now all of a sudden everyone's like, 'Wait a second. Why are we spending so much money on AI?'"* Airbnb looks at *"product velocity. How fast are you shipping? How many features … Are they high quality? And are they successful?"*

**7. Same tools, unequal results (17:53–20:03).** *"AI is the ultimate democracy. The tools that Airbnb has is the tools every company in the world has"*, like Photoshop for designers, *"but they're not equally distributed."* The gain is *"two things … the power of AI … but it's also cultural. People have to use the tools and they have to use them correctly."* *"AI is like an amplifier. It makes the good people better and the people that weren't as good, it's less of a multiplier for them. … The performance gap is greater with AI."* What matters now: *"it's no longer can you build it. It's what do you build and … should you build it."*

**8. Idea volume, and AI as the reader of his notebook (20:04–25:46).** Quoting Linus Pauling (*"the best way to have a good idea is to have a lot of ideas"*), he writes about a thousand words a day, bolds the lines that stand out, and *"at the end of every month … my AI now can just read the bold ideas … it can see patterns."* *"Good ideas don't die"*: they come back. Examples from Da Vinci, Van Gogh and Hemingway.

**9. Blinders, and the big screen (25:47–29:13).** Less time on X: *"now it's Instinct, now it's Muse … we're all like kids chasing soccer balls."* Ten to thirty minutes a day of keeping up is enough. *"Attention span is commensurate with screen size. … Do not look at a phone if you want to focus."*

**10. Chat is the wrong interface for most work (29:14–31:40).** If he were 26 now he would look at education, *"an AI Disney"* or health. Instinct and Muse are *"the beginning of consumer AI, agentic AI, but I do not think that the messaging interface is the right interface to do most work."* Fitting AI into messaging *"is like putting a jet engine on the back of a bicycle."*

**11. "There's going to be 50 more Anthropics, maybe a hundred" (31:40–34:05).** *"This is a cognitive revolution … We're not in a window. And if we are, it's a thousand-year window"*, with *"millions of small companies"* because *"one, two or three people can start a whole business."* On fear outside Silicon Valley: AI *"is changing their job. It's displacing them. And some of that may be true … But I am saying that everyone has the power now."* His own start with Claude Code: an employee tutored him. *"Jump in the pool … the water is not cold. It just looks cold."*

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **digital-seizing/rapid-prototyping** — services are tested in one city before rollout, and AI cut the time to ship a new service from a year to weeks.
- **strategic-renewal/business-model** — the stated move from a marketplace of homes to a services platform and *"travel community"*, with small service providers as the new supply side.
- **digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity** — the 80% gain is put down to tools *and* culture: Airbnb trained people to use the tools correctly, and dropped token counts for product-velocity metrics.

## Debates and supersession

- **Amplifier or leveller?** Chesky: *"the performance gap is greater with AI."* [[2026-04-28-brynjolfsson-li-raymond-generative-ai-at-work|Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond]] measured the reverse in customer support: the biggest gains for the least experienced. The two differ in evidence (a CEO's observation vs a field study) and in work (product design and engineering vs scripted support).
- **The 80% figure** has no baseline, method or definition of "feature". Treat it as a vendor claim.
