# Gartner 2027 CIO and Technology Executive Agenda survey: AI value (press release)

## TL;DR

Gartner's press release from its IT Symposium/Xpo in Orlando, 19 October 2026. VP Analyst Alicia Mullery presented the figures.

1. **13% report significant value.** *"Only 13% of CIOs reported their enterprise has realized significant or transformational value from AI so far."*
2. **Use cases grow, success does not.** *"While AI use cases have accelerated over the past two years, the likelihood of use case success remains one-in-five."*
3. **Stopping rules mark the leaders.** Organisations with rules for when a pilot should not go to production are **2.3 times** more likely to be "vanguards"; with rules for dropping low-value AI already in production, **2.6 times**. Only **31%** and **17%** of CIOs have these.
4. **Cost pressure, rising cost.** 58% of CIOs face more pressure to deliver cost improvement from AI; 51% expect AI to *raise* the total cost of ownership of technology.
5. **Work redesign is rare.** Plans to redesign roles for a human-agent workforce make an organisation **2.4 times** more likely to be a vanguard. Only **4%** of CIOs have such plans. 40% expect to increase IT headcount in 2027.

Mullery: *"Volume is not value."*

## Method and definitions

- **Survey:** the Gartner 2027 CIO and Technology Executive Agenda survey. **2,002 CIOs and technology executives**, May to July 2026. Countries, industries and firm sizes are not given in the release.
- **"Significant or transformational value"** is the respondent's own rating of their enterprise. The release gives no scale or question wording.
- **"Vanguards"** are *"CIOs who are delivering better performance on business outcomes while also reporting high CEO-perceived AI value."* The release does not give their share, and both parts are reported by the CIO.
- **"One-in-five"** is a likelihood of use-case success. The release does not say how success is defined or how the figure is computed. It is a different unit from the 13%: use cases, not CIOs.
- The 2.3x, 2.6x and 2.4x are cross-sectional associations, not effects.

## Scope and reliability

Self-report by technology executives about their own organisations. Gartner sells research and advice on these practices; the release points to a client-only note (*"Bridging the AI Trough"*). The sample is large, but question wording, sample composition and weighting are not given. Read the 13% as "CIOs who rate their AI value highly", not as a measured outcome.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: stopping rules at two points, pilot and production, are portfolio discipline in its plainest form.
- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`**: role redesign for a human-agent workforce, which only 4% of CIOs plan, and roles that "compress and expand."
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: the 13% and the vanguard group are a maturity reading of AI value, with new funding and governance models for probabilistic, run-time AI costs.
