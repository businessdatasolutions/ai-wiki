# The Emerging Agentic Enterprise (MIT SMR–BCG, November 2025)

## TL;DR

The **ninth** annual global study on AI and business strategy by [[MIT Sloan Management Review]] and [[Boston Consulting Group]], by [[Sam Ransbotham]], [[David Kiron]], [[Shervin Khodabandeh]], Sesh Iyer and Amartya Das. It is about agentic AI, not about maturity or value.

1. **Adoption, by type of AI.** Traditional AI adoption *"has climbed to 72%"* (2025). Generative AI reached 70% in three years. Agentic AI is at **35%**, with *"another 44%"* planning to deploy it soon (p. 7, Figure 1).
2. **The tool-coworker duality.** **76%** of respondents view agentic AI *"as more like a coworker than a tool"* (p. 5). The report builds four tensions on this: scalability vs adaptability, experience vs expediency, supervision vs autonomy, retrofit vs reengineer (pp. 8–9).
3. **Expected autonomy rises.** The share agreeing that AI systems have decision-making authority goes from 10% now to 35% expected in three years (+250%). Working independently from humans goes from 14% to 39% (p. 13, Figure 3).
4. **Organisational change expected by extensive adopters.** 66% expect operating-model changes (42% of non-adopters), 58% governance changes, 45% fewer middle-management layers, 43% plan to hire more generalists, 29% expect fewer entry-level roles (pp. 16–19, Appendix Figure 1).
5. **No maturity share.** The 2024 segmentation (15% "Augmented Learners") is **not repeated**. The report gives **no share of organisations achieving significant value** or financial benefit.

## Method and definitions

- **Sample.** A global survey fielded in spring 2025, *"records from 2,102 respondents representing more than 21 industries and 116 countries"*, plus interviews with 11 executives (p. 5). The report calls it a *"global executive survey"* (p. 5). It does not describe the sampling frame or response rate.
- **"Adoption"** in Figure 1 is the share of organisations *"piloting or deploying AI solutions"* (p. 7). Pilots count as adoption.
- **Segmentation.** Respondents are grouped by agentic-AI adoption: no plans, plans to adopt, piloting, some adoption, extensive adoption (appendix figures, pp. 22–33). The report does not give the share of respondents in each group, and it does not define "extensive".
- **Sponsor and authors.** *"In collaboration with and sponsored by Boston Consulting Group"* (p. 2). Khodabandeh, Iyer and Das are BCG partners or principals.

## Scope and reliability

- **Self-report**, by individual respondents about their organisation and their own jobs. Many figures are expectations ("in three years"), not observed outcomes.
- **No value measure.** Unlike the 2024 edition (15% Augmented Learners; financial-benefit questions), this edition reports no share of organisations getting significant value. It cannot be set beside the maturity bars of the thread as a newer measurement of the same thing.
- **Series continuity.** The adoption series is consistent with the 2024 report (50% in 2023, 70% in 2024). The 2025 sample (2,102 respondents, 116 countries) is smaller than 2024's (3,467, 136 countries).
- **Small print.** Reference 3 dates a WSJ story *"May 12, 2005"* (p. 35); the context places it in 2025.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-sensing/digital-scouting`**: an annual survey tracking how fast traditional, generative and agentic AI spread through organisations. Its sidebar reads the speed of agentic adoption through diffusion-of-innovations theory (p. 8).
- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: the "experience vs expediency" tension is an investment-timing and portfolio question. The report argues that standard net-present-value and depreciation models fail for systems that improve with use (pp. 11–12).
- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`**: the second half prescribes structural redesign: governance and decision rights, fewer management layers, dual career paths, and finance, HR and IT working jointly (pp. 15–21).
