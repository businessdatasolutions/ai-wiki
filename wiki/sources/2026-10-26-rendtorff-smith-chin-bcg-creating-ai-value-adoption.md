# Rendtorff-Smith & Chin (BCG) — creating AI value as adoption grows

> Smart AI development policies focused on society and the economy help advance innovation. The OECD's Sara Rendtorff-Smith and BCG's Vincent Chin discuss how.

## TL;DR

A five-minute interview on the [[Boston Consulting Group]] channel. **Vincent Chin** of BCG talks with **Sara Rendtorff-Smith**, who leads AI and emerging technologies at the **OECD**. It holds the wiki's most compact set of official figures on the adoption gap by firm size. Three claims:

1. **Safety and governance as enablers.** The tension between innovating and being safe is *"often presented as a tension,"* but *"safety, governance, policy is really an enabler for rolling out the technology."* The OECD recommends no single approach; around 80 countries have AI strategies, and its repository holds about 2,000 government documents.
2. **Money and use have both more than doubled.** VC funding for AI was about **$260 billion in 2025, 61% of all VC globally**, about double 2022. Firms using AI went from **8.7% to 20%** between 2023 and 2025.
3. **The gap is below the surface.** More than **50%** of big firms use AI against about **17%** of smaller firms. *"It's not just having access to the technology"*: the bottlenecks are skills, data and *"the organizational and managerial processes for integrating."* Then *"can you absorb it? As you absorb it, what value do you create?"*

## Key claims

- **Keeping pace.** Asked how to regulate something moving faster than policymakers' comprehension, she describes an iterative approach. Sandboxing is *"a nice balance,"* and Singapore's voluntary guidelines show another route. Partners across industry and academia bring the OECD the latest developments.
- **Value chain.** Three levels (firm, economy, society) read as a chain: enablers and investment, then diffusion, then value. Of value she says only *"there's still upside."*
- **Membership.** 38 member countries, and a partnership of 47 working on AI.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: skills, data and managerial processes named as what firms lack in order to absorb AI, measured by firm size.
