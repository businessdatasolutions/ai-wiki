# 2026 Global Health Care Outlook — Deloitte

> Health systems are prioritizing revenue, workforce challenges, and care model transformation as AI's transformative potential remains on the horizon.
>
> — the report's subtitle

## TL;DR

Deloitte's annual outlook for hospital systems, based on a survey of **180 C-suite executives of large health systems** in Australia, Canada, Germany, **the Netherlands**, the United Kingdom and the United States, fielded **August–September 2025**. AI is one of five themes; finance, workforce and cybersecurity are the others.

1. **Gen AI at scale in parts, rarely everywhere.** *"About 30% of surveyed health systems report operating gen AI at scale in select areas of their organizations … while just **2%** have deployed AI across their entire enterprise"* (introduction).
2. **Returns are mostly unmeasured.** **51%** *"either haven't measured returns or determined that it is too soon to see results"*; **31%** report *"moderate"* financial returns; **3%** say the ROI has been *"significant"* (section "AI will likely be important in 2026").
3. **Expectations are high anyway.** In Figure 3 (n = 178), **56%** expect *"significant value"* from gen AI and agentic AI for operational efficiencies in 2026, and **52%** for administrative tasks such as ambient scribes.
4. **AI is not the top 2026 priority.** Only **22%** of non-US and **37%** of US executives say AI is likely to be a major focus at their facilities in 2026. Workforce is the top concern; more than 90% call productivity a priority.
5. **Deloitte's own advice names the pilot trap.** *"Many organizations pursue pilots or proofs of concept but stumble when trying to scale."*

## Method and definitions

- **Sample.** 180 C-suite executives *"from large health systems"*, run by Deloitte Global with the Deloitte US Center for Health Solutions through an external survey vendor. Figure 2 gives n = 120 for non-US respondents, so about 60 were US executives (inferred). No per-country counts, no Dutch subsample, no response rate.
- **Fieldwork.** August and September 2025.
- **Definitions.** "At scale in select areas" and "across their entire enterprise" are not defined further. Both are the executive's own judgement. ROI is a self-rated category (significant / moderate / not measured or too soon); no financial threshold is given.
- **Sponsor.** Deloitte, a consultancy that sells health-care AI and transformation services.

## Scope and reliability

- **Self-report by executives**, small N per country (on average 30), developed markets only. The report says so: *"the insights primarily reflect the perspectives of developed health care markets."*
- **Two budget figures disagree.** The text says executives expect gen AI and agentic AI to take 19% of technology budgets; Figure 2 gives AI/Gen AI 14% for non-US respondents. They may describe different bases; the page does not reconcile them.
- **The Netherlands is in the sample but not reported separately.** No figure on the page is specific to Dutch health systems.
- **A web report, not a PDF.** The "Download PDF" button prints the page; there is no longer report behind it.
