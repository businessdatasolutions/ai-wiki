# Fowler & Ludlow (Bloomberg Tech) — the AI gold rush on college campuses

> Dorm Room Fund says fears that AI will make college obsolete miss a startup boom already unfolding on campuses. The student-focused venture fund is seeing growing optimism among young founders as AI lowers barriers to building companies and gives first-time entrepreneurs new opportunities. Molly Fowler, founding partner at Dorm Room Fund, joins Ed Ludlow on "Bloomberg Tech."

## TL;DR

A five-minute *Bloomberg Tech* segment ([[Bloomberg Podcasts|Bloomberg]]), published 16 October 2026. Host **Ed Ludlow** interviews **Molly Fowler**, founding partner of **Dorm Room Fund** (DRF), a venture fund started in 2012 that backs students and recent graduates. Ludlow frames it against *"this growing debate over whether college is still worth it in the age of AI as the technology threatens to reshape entry level jobs."* Three claims:

1. **Big technology shifts favour first-time builders on campus.** *"When there are massive technology shifts… the macro trends privilege first time builders and folks that are on campus."* A university is *"the ultimate hacker house or accelerator"*: concentrated talent, time to build, teaching at the leading edge.
2. **Students scout students.** DRF has about 60 student investors around the US who find *"their most talented teammates and colleagues way before the rest of the ecosystem."* Of about 1,500 applicants a year it takes about 30.
3. **College is not going anywhere, but its form is changing.** Applications to top schools hit records, the gap between *"the very top schools and those that are not… keeping up"* may widen, and student organisations where people *"help each other build"* (Socratica, Dorm Room Fund) are growing.

## Key claims

### The Cursor example

*"Michael, the CEO of [[Cursor]]"* (Michael Truell) *"was a member of our student investing team"* while a sophomore at MIT. DRF knew him for three years, backed him when he started building and *"helped introduce him to some of his early capital."* The early cap table *"is dotted with dorm room fund alumni,"* many of whom became investors or founders.

### Students and the four-year degree

Ludlow points out that leaving university to found a company is not new (Zuckerberg left Harvard). Fowler describes a *"more mercenary attitude"* since the pandemic: talented students still go to top universities *"for the credentialing… for their peers,"* but may stay *"two or three years as opposed to all four."* On whether college is worth it in the AI age: colleges, MBA, master's and PhD programmes remain *"an incredibly dense place for talent,"* and *"as a concept, it's not going anywhere."*
