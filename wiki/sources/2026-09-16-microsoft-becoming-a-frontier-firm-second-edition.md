# Microsoft — Becoming a Frontier Firm: Our Frontier Playbook (second edition)

## TL;DR

A 44-page slide deck from [[Microsoft]], titled *Becoming a Frontier Firm, second edition*, with the subtitle *"Our Frontier Playbook: Microsoft's evolving methodology for driving AI transformation, and what it might mean for your company."* The launch date in the file name is 16 September 2026. It carries no byline. The conclusion is signed by **Kathleen Hogan**, EVP Strategy and Transformation, and **Katy George**, CVP AI & Work Transformation. This is the *Microsoft Frontier Playbook* that George presented at [[2026-11-19-george-hrncirik-ignite-becoming-a-frontier-firm|Ignite 2026]]; the session's description links this file. The cover disclaims that the playbook shares *"our working approach… not to prescribe a solution nor guarantee results for others."*

It is built around **five elements**:

| | Element | Stated aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Set business ambition & goals | Anchor AI in service to your business strategy and creating enterprise value |
| B | Build your diffusion engine | Build an AI operating model that delivers against your business goals at scale |
| C | Invest in your people | Adapt roles, manager behaviors, and learning so AI expands human capability instead of eroding it |
| D | Codify your advantage & controls | Codify what makes your company distinct into evals and a learning system, then keep that intelligence compounding inside your boundary |
| E | Safeguard your security | Apply enterprise-grade governance to every agent action before you scale |

What the Ignite session did not have:

- an organisational design (AI Transformation Offices per function, plus a small central *"learning accelerator"*);
- three **levels of AI** across the recipes;
- the formula **CI + AI = CA**;
- a four-layer **measurement framework** with impact timelines;
- a people section it calls *"the most important"*;
- the **hill-climbing machine** built on private evals;
- a security section.

## Key claims

### A. Set business ambition and goals

AI transformation *"is not a product launch, nor a tech project."* Every initiative is anchored to the four goals of the **Frontier Firm Success Framework**, each with example outcome metrics:

| Goal | What Microsoft is doing | Example outcome metrics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enrich employee experiences | AI in the flow of work, learning prompts in daily routines; heaviest AI users report the highest "Thrive" scores | Engagement ("Thriving"), hours reclaimed per employee per week, accuracy of self-service, time to proficiency, skill acquisition rate |
| Reinvent customer engagement | Sales Chat, a Coaching agent, a Deal agent, a Sales agent that closes small accounts; support agents routing to the best human engineer | Revenue per sales rep, qualified pipeline coverage, customer lifetime value, net revenue retention, conversion, NPS/CSAT, win rate |
| Reshape business processes | End-to-end process reinvention in every corporate function; a Customer Zero Council | End-to-end cycle time, risk events, customer experience, cost per transaction, quality and error rate, audit exceptions |
| Bend the curve on innovation | Better products, not only faster coding; AI in data-center operations | Time from idea to market, customer success, developer productivity and experience, experimentation velocity, patents per R&D dollar |

The processes section gives *"two watchouts from our own experience"*:

1. *"push ourselves to work customer-back and end-to-end… not reinforcing the siloes of our org chart"*;
2. *"redesign the work rather than simply automate it."*

**Organisation.** Each function and business area has its own **AI Transformation Office (AITO)**, which brings together technical, process and people capabilities. Each AITO also governs its area: enlisting citizen developers, managing risk, prioritising scale-up. A *"small centralized team"* acts as a *"learning accelerator"* across the AITOs and supports four enablers: People, Methodology (the recipes, using continuous-improvement tools), Technology Enablement, and Measurement.

**Three learnings:**

- *"Cascade goals from the top, not use cases from the bottom… Resist letting bottom-up activity become the strategic narrative."*
- *"Pair every frontier goal with a named executive owner."*
- *"Force yourselves past efficiency."* Efficiency, speed, quality and cost are *"nonnegotiable"*; experience, innovation and growth *"are the ceiling."*

### B. Build your diffusion engine

*"A tool licensed and rolled out to 100,000 employees does not change how the work gets done."* What changes it is *"the operating system around the tool: the rituals, the redesign, the measurement, the manager behavior, the learning loops, the culture of experimentation."* The method comes from a review of *"over 100 case studies of internal AI transformation."* Its main lesson: AI diffusion cannot be treated *"as a tech product launch or an IT project."* Continuous-improvement rigor often finds *"significant opportunities for improvement even before we get to applying new AI prompts and agents."* Microsoft calls this work *"a 'new science of knowledge work'"*, because knowledge work is *"tacit, undocumented, nonstandard."*

The **three recipes** each come with "what matters most in practice":

- **Persona acceleration: *"Start with the role, not the tool."*** The gap between power users and the rest *"is rarely access"*; Microsoft calls it *"the jagged edge—human version."*
  - What matters: identify personas and make the role visible (time across meetings, admin, focus and customer work, with benchmarks); map AI to real moments (*"Do not ask people to 'use AI more'"*); create role-based learning loops (small-group huddles); engage direct managers visibly; instrument the change.
  - In sales, the pilot combined weekly huddles, telemetry and AI best practices codified into *"Experience Cards."* The figures are the Ignite slide's: 3x self-reported adoption, and 9.4% revenue per head and 20% higher close rates *within the pilot group*.
- **AI-powered process redesign: *"Optimize your workflows before introducing agents."*** Persona gains *"often evaporate—speeding up one step frequently just creates longer queues and larger work backlogs elsewhere,"* and *"dropping an AI agent into the existing process often just automates the dysfunction."*
  - What matters: map the workflow end to end (gemba, process mining, task decomposition, user research, interviews; *"not how the org chart and process maps says it happens"*); de-waste before automating; build a shared data foundation; design the human-agent operating model deliberately, *"calibrated to the stakes of each decision"*; use cross-functional squads with named owners; build for reuse.
  - Supply chain went *"lean before agents"*, built a single source of truth, then deployed *"111 purpose-built agents across planning, sourcing, fulfillment, and logistics."* Cycle time fell 75% *"across selected workflows"*; the efficiency and incremental-value results again carry no figure.
- **AI-first possibility: *"Start from a blank page."*** This is *"the incubator recipe"*. It is *"not the first recipe most organizations scale broadly, but it is often where the most important breakthroughs emerge."*
  - What matters: a small expert squad with *"judgment and taste"* and benchmarks to judge AI output against; zero-base the work and make AI the default route; sandbox governance; an agent-friendly operating model, with standards, prompts, evals and skills *"where agents can learn from them"*; remove *"velocity crushers"*.
  - *"The prize is not only the productivity number. The prize is the reusable pattern: the spec, prompt, eval, skill, agent behavior, operating rule, or architecture pattern."*
  - The Copilot Cowork squad *"truly 'zero-based' the work by taking an 'automate everything' mindset and implementing a spec-driven development approach"*: nine people, 18.6K commits, 9.3M lines of code, first release in 35 days. Each sandbox feeds an *"innovation flywheel"*: experimentation, codify learnings into a shared library and guardrails, scale the model.

**Three levels of AI** run across all three recipes:

1. *human with assistant*;
2. *human-agent teams* (*"Agents join the team, taking on specific tasks with human direction"*);
3. *human-led, agent-operated* (*"agents run entire business processes and workflows, checking in as needed"*).

The level is chosen by risk boundaries, data and technology readiness, and organisational readiness. Level 3 *"requires a much bigger role-change for humans."* One recipe can use several levels at once.

**CI + AI = CA (Capability Add).** Continuous-improvement levers *eliminate*: errors and rework, unused outputs, delays and queues, handoffs and context loss, tool-switching. AI's Capability Add *adds*: predictive and preventive quality intelligence, an expanded solution space, real-time sensemaking and parallel cognition, opportunity sensing and portfolio intelligence, attention and cognitive-load optimisation, precision at the right level of abstraction. *"CI alone gives you a leaner version of your old company. CI + AI… compounds."*

**Measuring the pipeline from adoption to impact.** Four layers, each with an impact timeline:

| Layer | Measures | Timeline | Sales example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Input | Activity: adoption, readiness; who uses AI, where, how often | 2-4 weeks | AI sales tool adoption, user satisfaction (survey) |
| Throughput | Leading process indicators: how AI is applied and integrated | 1-3 months | % time with customers, % time on AI platform products |
| Output | Productivity and performance | 3-6 months | Qualified pipeline, win rate |
| Outcome | The Success Framework goals | 6+ months | Revenue per account executive, customer success |

The dependencies between the layers are mapped explicitly. *"If input metrics are healthy but throughput metrics aren't moving in three to six months, we know we have a habit-formation problem, not a tooling problem."*

### C. Invest in your people

*"This may be the most important section."* George, quoted: *"We found that we initially focused too much on technology alone, when the biggest effort we needed to make is with people."* And: *"human capital does not become less valuable as token capital grows. It only becomes more valuable."* Four areas:

1. **Engage employees with honesty and optimism.** Staying silent is not safer. Leaders should be explicit about creating *"great jobs and career ladders"*, engage teams in redesigning their own work (*"they are the experts!"*), *"focus more on adding AI capability into workflows than on simply taking human capacity out,"* and *"keep critical people-related decisions human."*
2. **Reimagine the people model.**
   - Roles become T-shaped: engineers become *"full-stack product builders"* rather than *"master coders"*, and sales reps *"content integrators."*
   - *"Manager role-modeling is the single biggest predictor of adoption"*; managers who *"delegate AI use to the team do not move their organizations."*
   - Higher-order skills (judgment and taste, business acumen, scoping and delegating work *"including to agents"*, learning agility) get *"a higher bar sooner."*
   - Teams become more fluid, with Forward Deployed Engineering teams doing *"Hypervelocity Engineering."*
   - *"Early career roles should be redesigned, not automated away"*: automating junior work hollows out *"the apprenticeship pipeline that produces senior talent."* The PRAISE programme pairs new engineers with mentors: *"the senior teaches craft; the junior teaches AI fluency."*
   - Operating models shift *"from function to value stream"*; incentives should reward impact over tenure, and span-and-layer rules be relaxed.
   - The side panels cite the *Work Trend Index, May 2026*: *"+17 pts lift in recognized AI value when managers actively model AI use"*; *"50% AI users identified quality control and … 46% … critical thinking as the most important human skills"* (elliptical on the page); *"49% of AI work is cognitive support"*; *"2x impact on AI effectiveness from organizational interventions than focus on individual mindset and skill."*
3. **Reinvent change management**, from *"the classroom-and-rollout model"* to learning in the flow of work: peer practitioner-led, team-based, Learn-Do-Teach, *"a permanent part of the new rhythm of business."* The examples are Camp AIR (a three-week bootcamp, *"leave their disciplines at the door"*) and weekly sales huddles led by power users.
4. **Give employees agency**: LinkedIn's skills and job-trend data (its top five fastest-growing US skills are AI literacy, conflict mitigation, adaptability, process optimisation, innovative thinking), an AI talent marketplace, and learning in the flow of work through Viva Learning.

### D. Codify your advantage and controls

*"If we had to name one of the most undervalued moves a top team can make this year, it would be this: codify what makes your company distinct in a form AI can learn from—and protect."* A firm's advantage is tacit, and *"at risk of being averaged away."* **Private evals** turn the *"secret sauce"* into *"concrete, testable, and repeatable"* standards. The next step is a reinforcement-learning environment, the **hill-climbing machine**: evals define good, context and harness steer the work, and each cycle of feedback and tuning improves the system.

The *"law of hill-climbing"*: *"Any enterprise whose competitive advantage cannot be measured by its own evals, improved through its own expertise, and preserved across any foundation model will be arbitraged."* The stack, from top to bottom:

- security and governance;
- the RL environment: evals (tasks, rubrics), agent, runtimes;
- context and harness (multi-model, Enterprise IQ, memory, skills, MCP);
- interchangeable models.

This *"separates what you own and differentiate—your evals, context, and control layer—from what you consume externally."*

The deck names **four dimensions** of the secret sauce: point of view, performance (proprietary data and IP), taste, guardrails and risk. It adds **four factors** for safeguarding the machine: hill-climbing infrastructure, data residency, model independence, tenant isolation (*"Do identity, access, compliance, and policy controls apply to agents the same way they apply to users?"*). Nadella is quoted: customers increasingly *"want to be able to capture the tacit knowledge they possess inside of model weights as their core IP. This is probably the most important sovereign consideration for firms."*

### E. Safeguard your security

Four dimensions, each a question and an answer:

- **Defend with AI**: move to *"continuous, adaptive AI protection"* powered by a Security Graph.
- **Safely enable AI**: give agents identity and risk-based permissions, and govern their full lifecycle.
- **Trust AI with your data**: use AI for data classification and to remove posture drift; *"There is no trusting AI without securing your data."*
- **Get ready for AI**: *"Close the door before agents find it"*, through exposure management, patching, identity and network isolation.

The conclusion asks for agent guardrails so that *"AI actions are traceable, auditable, and reversible."*

### Conclusion

*"We are still early in this journey… We will get some of this wrong, and you might too."* It ends with an invitation to partner with *"Microsoft's Frontier Company"* on your own transformation.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`.** An AI Transformation Office in every function with a central learning accelerator. Operating models shift *"from function to value stream"*. Squads have named owners, and span-and-layer guidelines are relaxed.
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`.** The diffusion engine itself: three recipes, three levels of AI, and a measurement framework from input to outcome. Reskilling happens in the flow of work through Camp AIR, huddles and the Skill Advisor agent.
- **`digital-seizing/rapid-prototyping`.** The AI-first recipe runs small expert squads in sandboxes, and each sandbox publishes its patterns to a shared library through an *"innovation flywheel"*.
- **`contextual/internal-enablers`.** A named executive owner for every goal, goals cascaded from the top, manager role-modelling as *"the single biggest predictor of adoption"*, and recognition through AI Pioneers.
- **`strategic-renewal/organizational-culture`.** Change management becomes *"a new culture of continuous experimentation, adaptation, and learning,"* tied to Microsoft's Growth Mindset culture and Model-Coach-Care. Honesty about uncertainty and *"keep critical people-related decisions human."*

## Scope and reliability

A vendor's account of its own transformation. It is published to market the products it describes and closes with a sales contact. It disclaims prescription and guaranteed results, and it says Microsoft is *"not at the end of that journey."* The results come from Microsoft's own pilots, under the same footer as the Ignite slides (*"impact that we are seeing with the recipe approach, in the context of our company"*), with no method, sample or comparison group. The Work Trend Index figures are Microsoft's own survey research, cited without a sample or question wording, and one of them is cut off on the page. The frameworks (Success Framework, recipes, levels, CI + AI = CA, the measurement layers, the hill-climbing machine) are useful as vocabulary. They are not findings. Sections D and E argue for an architecture that Microsoft sells: tenant, evals, data residency, agent identity.
