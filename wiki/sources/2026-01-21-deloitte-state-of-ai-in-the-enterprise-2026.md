# Deloitte, State of AI in the Enterprise 2026: "The untapped edge"

## TL;DR

The Deloitte AI Institute's annual survey, January 2026. 3,235 director-to-C-suite leaders in 24 countries, surveyed August to September 2025. The Netherlands is in the sample (n=50).

1. **A quarter have scaled pilots.** *"Only 25% of respondents said their organization has moved 40% or more of their AI experiments into production to date"*; 54% expect to reach that level within three to six months (p. 4, p. 8).
2. **A fifth govern agents maturely.** *"1 in 5 (21%) report having a mature model for governance of autonomous agents"* (p. 6, p. 20), while 74% expect to use agentic AI at least moderately within two years, against 23% today (p. 17).
3. **A quarter report transformative effect.** 25% of leaders say AI is having a *"transformative effect"* on their company, up from 12% a year earlier (p. 10). Only 20% are growing revenue through AI today; 74% hope to (p. 10).
4. **Three thirds of depth.** 34% are *"starting to use AI to deeply transform"* the business, 30% are redesigning key processes, and 37% use AI *"at a surface level"* with little or no process change (p. 4).
5. **Jobs are not redesigned.** 84% of companies have not redesigned jobs around AI, and insufficient worker skills are the biggest barrier to integrating AI (p. 5, p. 13).

## Method and definitions

- **Sample:** 3,235 leaders, *"director-level to C-suite-level,"* including board members, presidents and vice presidents. Half IT leaders, half line-of-business leaders. Six industries, 24 countries. Fieldwork August to September 2025. Fifteen executive interviews added (pp. 7, 40).
- **Countries:** the United States is the largest group (n=1,200, 37%). **Netherlands n=50.** Others include the UK (220), India (200), Canada (175), Germany (170), France (150) (p. 40).
- **Selection:** *"All participating organizations have one or more working implementations of AI being used daily"* (p. 40). The sample is AI users, not all firms. Respondents had to influence or make AI decisions, or oversee implementations.
- **The 25% question:** *"In your estimation, what percentage of your AI experiments (e.g., pilots, test cases, etc.) have been deployed to date into your organization (moved into production)?"* (p. 8). The threshold of 40% is Deloitte's cut-off on that estimate.
- **"Mature model for governance"** of agents is not defined beyond the label.
- **Unit:** each figure is a share of respondents answering for their organisation. With one respondent per organisation this is close to a share of organisations; the report does not say whether that holds.

## Scope and reliability

Self-report by AI decision-makers in organisations that already use AI daily. That selection pushes adoption figures up, and makes the low shares (25%, 21%) more striking, not less. Deloitte sells AI services, and the report closes with actions for leaders. The sample is large, the country breakdown is published, the key question is printed, and the series runs year on year. The Dutch press release (21 January 2026) gives the 21% as *"only 21% of those companies"*, meaning those planning agentic AI; the report gives it as 21% of companies surveyed (p. 6, p. 20).

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-seizing/balancing-digital-portfolios`**: the pilot-to-production rate is a portfolio measure; the Dutch press release calls its absence *"pilot fatigue."*
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: the split into deep transformation (34%), process redesign (30%) and surface use (37%) is a three-level maturity reading.
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`**: insufficient worker skills are named the biggest barrier, and 84% have not redesigned jobs around AI.
