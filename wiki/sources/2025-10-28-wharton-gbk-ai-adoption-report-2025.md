# Accountable Acceleration: Wharton/GBK 2025 AI Adoption Report

## TL;DR

The third wave of an annual survey of US enterprise decision makers by Wharton Human-AI Research and GBK Collective. About 800 people answered between 26 June and 11 July 2025 (PDF p. 6). Its 74% is often set against MIT NANDA's 95%.

1. **Three in four report a positive return.** **74%** of all respondents say their organisation's GenAI ROI to date is positive: **35%** "significantly positive" and **39%** "moderately positive" (PDF p. 47; text, p. 38). The base is all respondents (n=801), not only firms that measure ROI.
2. **Most say they measure it.** **72%** say their organisation measures GenAI ROI in a fully integrated (37%) or consistent, standardised (35%) way (PDF p. 45; p. 7).
3. **Use is now routine.** **82%** use GenAI at least weekly (+10 points on 2024) and **46%** daily (+17 points) (PDF p. 7).
4. **The largest firms are least sure.** In Tier 1 firms ($2B+ revenue), 57% report positive ROI and **25%** say it is *"too early to tell/still in pilot phase"* (PDF p. 47).
5. **Seniority shapes the answer.** 81% of VP-and-above see positive ROI against 69% of managers and directors (PDF p. 12, p. 48).
6. **People are the stated bottleneck.** 89% agree GenAI enhances employees' skills, and **43%** see a risk of declining skill proficiency (PDF p. 7).

## Method and definitions

- **Instrument** (PDF p. 6): a 15-minute online quantitative tracking survey, United States only. *"Interviews conducted between June 26 and July 11, 2025."* Respondents: ~800 in 2025 (n=801 on the charts), ~800 in 2024, ~670 in 2023.
- **Who answered.** Senior decision makers in HR, IT, Legal, Marketing/Sales, Operations, Product/Engineering, Purchasing/Procurement, Finance/Accounting or General Management. Their employers are US commercial organisations with 1,000+ employees and more than $50 million in revenue (PDF p. 6). Charts split them into Tier 3 ($50M–<$250M, n=218), Tier 2 ($250M–<$2B, n=428) and Tier 1 ($2B+, n=155).
- **Unit.** People, not firms. Each answer is one decision maker's view of their organisation. There is no firm-level deduplication or financial data.
- **What counts as a "positive return".** A perception gathered through *"internal conversations with colleagues and senior leadership"*. "Moderately positive" is defined as *"measurable benefits, but limited or in early stages"*, which is a low bar. The question is new in 2025, so there is no earlier ROI figure to compare (PDF p. 47).
- **Repeated cross-section.** Questions and answer options changed between waves, and *"certain questions cannot be tracked across each year"* (PDF p. 88).
- **Sponsor and fieldwork.** The study *"is sponsored by Wharton Human-AI Research ...; GBK Collective performed research and analysis"*. GBK led the 2023 wave with Puntoni, and the joint study began in 2024 (PDF p. 4). GBK describes itself as a *"marketing strategy and insights consultancy"* (PDF p. 4). Korst, a GBK partner, is also *"founder of Mindspan Labs—a forward-thinking AI consultancy and incubator"* (PDF p. 3).

## Scope and reliability

- **Self-report by optimistic respondents.** Every figure is a decision maker's own judgement. The report shows that seniority moves the answer: VP-and-above are twice as likely as managers to say their organisation adopts *"much faster"* than others (56% vs 28%, PDF p. 10).
- **Generous ROI definition.** Puntoni told Big Technology: *"Our definition is very different from the MIT report. Theirs is much more stringent."* Big Technology adds that the Wharton study counted individual productivity, employee retention and operational efficiency (Kantrowitz, 31 Oct 2025; raw at `raw/articles/2025-10-31-kantrowitz-big-technology-are-74-percent-of-businesses-seeing-genai-roi.md`).
- **Base.** Big Technology wrote that 74% *"of businesses that measure the ROI"* see a positive return. The chart's base is all respondents (*"Among Total"*, n=801). The executive summary's sentence *"Most firms now measure ROI, and roughly three in four already see positive returns"* (PDF p. 8) invites the narrower reading.
- **Sample frame.** US only. "Enterprise" starts at $50 million in revenue, so more than a quarter of respondents work at firms below $250 million.
- **Who paid.** Big Technology reports that *"Wharton paid for the study"* and describes GBK as *"a company that does some AI advising"*. The report itself names no commercial sponsor.

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-sensing/digital-scouting`**: a yearly scan of how US enterprises take up GenAI, by function, industry, firm size and seniority.
- **`digital-transforming/improving-digital-maturity`**: the human-capital section measures skills (89% enhance, 43% atrophy risk), training budgets, hiring and Chief AI Officer roles (PDF pp. 58–81).
- **`contextual/internal-barriers`**: the top barriers are security risk, operational complexity and data inaccuracy. Lack of training resources enters the top ten. "Laggards" cite employee resistance and lack of trust more often (PDF pp. 39, 57).
