# From Event to Ecosystem: Rethinking How Technology Leaders Build Knowledge in the AI Era

> Grand Ballroom, West Tower, Floor 2
> 1:00 PM - 1:30 PM: Keynote
> Speaker: Allan Tate, Executive Chair, MIT Sloan CIO Symposium
>
> Description: Traditional conferences and publications are no longer sufficient for leaders navigating rapid, uncertain change. As AI reshapes how decisions are made and work is organized, CIOs and data leaders need new ways to continuously interpret emerging technologies and translate insight into action, This session introduces a practical model for evolving from one-time events to year-round knowledge ecosystems—combining structured inquiry, peer dialogue, and real-time experimentation. Drawing on the MIT Sloan CIO community, it highlights how leaders can strengthen judgment, design more effective organizations, and move from individual productivity gains to enterprise-level value. The goal is not faster answers, but better questions.
>
> *— MIT Sloan CIO Symposium Videos YouTube channel*

A **~19-minute pre-event keynote** opening the **23rd annual MIT Sloan CIO Symposium** (2026 theme: *"Human AI synergy — redefining leadership for a transformative future"*), delivered by **[[Allan Tate]]** (Executive Chair, [[MIT Sloan CIO Symposium]]; ASR renders his name "Alan Tate"). Tate and **[[Irving Wladawsky-Berger]]** also co-host the symposium's weekly podcast, including [[2026-07-10-when-ai-becomes-common-organizational-capability-becomes-the-advantage-ep-16|Episode 16]] on organizational capability as the AI-era advantage. Unlike the wiki's other agentic-AI-deployment sources, this talk is not about deploying AI inside a firm — it is the symposium's own account of how a **professional leadership community** restructured its own knowledge-production model in response to AI, making it a rare *meta-level* instance of the [[dynamic-capabilities]] sensing/seizing/transforming logic applied to a community rather than a single organization.

## The symposium's own history as a sensing timeline

Tate frames the talk with the symposium's 20-year arc as a proxy for the wider adoption curve: **2005** — understanding technology, e-business experimentation, the early internet. **2011** — driving transformation; Watson's Jeopardy win, cloud/mobile/analytics adoption, alongside early unintended consequences. **2018** — scaling AI and digital; platforms, ecosystems, enterprise AI; "each wave increased speed, complexity, and leadership demands." His diagnosis of what's different this time: prior technologies "transform physical work and information flow," but **cognitive technologies reshape how thinking itself is supported and distributed** — and "the problem is no longer adoption of technology. The problem today is orchestration of technology." AI is becoming general-purpose infrastructure, and technology decisions are now leadership decisions.

## The inquiry framework: from debates to structured inquiry

Tate names the throughline problem: **"leadership demands are outpacing our ability to learn"** — most learning models are built for stability, not rapid change, so the symposium itself had to change how it produces knowledge (adding podcasts, discussions, and inquiry sessions; moving from a one-time annual event toward continuous learning). In 2024 the symposium experimented with debates (useful for critical-thinking pedagogy, but not for what CIOs need); it pivoted instead to an **inquiry framework** — a seven-session series for senior leaders built on **fundamental questions, multiple interpretive lenses, and no forced conclusions**, aiming to improve *how leaders think*, not *what they think*.

Origin story: the series began by accident, when Tate and **Irving Wladawsky-Berger** (dangling; ASR badly garbles the name as "Irving Vladaskki Burgerer") were discussing an AI-consciousness article Tate felt was being covered "more for clicks and hype" than substance — and decided to build a different kind of engagement around inquiry rather than certainty, rather than argue about it online.

The **structured approach** has three steps: (1) **frame the question**, often via scenarios; (2) **examine competing interpretations and tensions** — frequently the differing views of different actors within the enterprise; (3) **translate insights into decisions** — primarily, what is the impact to the CIO. The first inquiry series ran six structured questions, moving from fundamental ("how do machines approach problems they have not previously encountered?") to applied ("how should machine and human intelligence be compared?" — as complementary capabilities, e.g. AI drafts/humans edit; or overlapping capabilities, e.g. cybersecurity or medicine, where human and machine reach the same task by different paths and different constraints).

## Four insights from the first inquiry series

- **AI is a design question, not primarily a capability question.** The same technology produces different results depending on organizational structure, decision processes, and human-machine interaction design — Tate cites the symposium's own use of structured prompts and task specifications to get repeatable results from AI on repetitive tasks. This echoes the wiki's existing interaction-design finding ([[2025-06-09-krakowski-human-centered-ai-field-experiment|Krakowski et al.]]) and framing-matters finding ([[2026-05-06-kropp-bcg-hbr-dont-treat-ai-agents-like-employees|Kropp et al.]]) on [[enterprise-ai-adoption]].
- **A normative test for what counts as success**: *"if you increase production but you decrease human understanding, I think that's a failure, because you're surrendering to the machine. But if you increase production and at the same time you increase the human understanding of your team… then you enhance the human experience, and that's what we think of as success."* A crisp criterion distinct from — but compatible with — the [[micro-productivity-trap]]'s workflow-redesign escape.
- **The constraint shifts from execution to prioritization.** AI increases the output of ideas and possibilities, so the live question stops being "what to do" and becomes "what not to do." Tate names the symposium's own first AI-assisted annual report (produced last year) as a concrete instance: *"as intelligence becomes abundant, judgment and imagination is what becomes scarce."*
- **AI expands the scope of work before it reduces it.** More initiatives, not fewer; increased complexity; redefinition of roles; a structural shift requiring more coordination and accountability. Tate names the inquiry series itself as a human-machine collaboration his staff could not have produced alone.

## Governance as a design question, and the redefined CIO role

Governance, in Tate's framing, "is not just about control or compliance. It defines who or what decides, and when, and with whose authority" — and governance/fiduciary responsibility drives institutional trust, which drives brand equity and customer adoption. (He flags a symposium session the next day on "governance as strategy" from **[[Michael Schrage]]** — ASR renders the name "Michael Shre.") Leadership, in this reading, means managing **persistent tensions rather than solving them**: human judgment vs. machine optimization, assistance vs. autonomy, efficiency vs. ambition, speed vs. understanding.

Across the seven inquiry sessions the symposium's own view of AI shifted from *capability* ("what can AI do?") to *design* ("how do we design organizations in a world with abundant intelligence?") — and with it, the CIO's role: **"a designer of intelligence systems, an owner of decision frameworks, an enforcer of prioritization discipline, and an architect of governance."** (Tate also flags a session on the changing role of the CIO from Irving Wladawsky-Berger.) Open questions the symposium names for itself: the limits of autonomy, how to measure value, new and evolving risks, and how to optimize the operational model.

## From one-day event to year-round knowledge ecosystem

The talk's closing frame is the symposium's own knowledge-delivery evolution as a small, self-referential adoption case: **recordings only** (historical) → **recordings + session synopsis + transcript** (last year) → **recordings + synopsis + transcript + a synthesis of the entire event** (this year) → a planned future **search-driven UI** serving video clips and transcript snippets on demand — moving attendees from "watching the videos" to *"interact[ing] with a collective intelligence of our community."* The stated ambition: become a **trusted conveyor of knowledge**, provide a **shared interpretation** among leaders, sustain a **continuous learning model**, and offer **collective intelligence as a capability** — a shift from *consuming insights* to *producing shared understanding*. The next inquiry series will explore "the future of human flourishing": human capabilities, judgment, and development, and how organizational and social behaviors should evolve. Tate closes on a historical framing — the printing press disrupted authority, industrialization disrupted labor, broadcast media disrupted culture, the internet destabilized information — and names **trust** as the MIT Sloan CIO community's differentiator going forward.
