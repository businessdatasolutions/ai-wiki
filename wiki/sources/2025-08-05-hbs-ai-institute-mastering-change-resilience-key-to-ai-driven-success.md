# HBS AI Institute — Mastering change resilience: the key to AI-driven success

## TL;DR

An ~870-word summary, published 5 August 2025 by the [[HBS AI Institute]], of a *Harvard Business Review* article by [[Karim Lakhani]] (HBS; the institute's co-founder and faculty chair), Jen Stave (the institute's executive director), Doug Ng (its director of design) and Daniel Martines (BCG X): *A Guide to Building Change Resilience in the Age of AI* (29 July 2025). The argument:

1. **The gap is organisational, not technical.** *"The primary obstacle is the ability of companies to adapt, reinvent, and scale new ways of working. We call this change resilience."* A BCG survey cited in the article finds *"just 26% of organizations have achieved value from AI."*
2. **The old model was episodic.** *"You modernized your systems, trained your people, and operated in a stable environment until the next wave of disruption hit."* AI makes that cycle too slow; change has to become a standing competency.
3. **Three "muscles":**
   - **Sensing** — *"to pick up weak technological, competitive, or societal signals early."*
   - **Rewiring** — *"the capacity to redeploy talent, data, capital, and decision rights in days or weeks, not fiscal quarters."*
   - **Lock-in** — *"the discipline to codify what a team learns (in process, code, or policy) so the next initiative starts from a higher baseline instead of reinventing the wheel."*
4. **Five components of a playbook:** **Learn** (widespread experimentation to shift attitudes and find opportunities), **Do** (fast initiatives against known deficiencies), **Imagine** (invent new operating models *"instead of duck-taping existing ones"*), **Act** (make the cycle continuous), **Care** (wellbeing measures against burnout).
5. **Exemplars:** Shopify, which spun off its logistics arm in 2023 to concentrate on product and built AI-native tools such as Sidekick; and Accenture, DBS, Moderna, P&G and Cisco.

The summary's own conclusion: the firms with breakthrough AI results *"invest in their organization's capacity to change as aggressively as they invest in AI technology itself."*

## Dynamic-capabilities reading

- **`digital-sensing/digital-scouting`** — *sensing* is defined as picking up weak technological, competitive and societal signals early.
- **`digital-seizing/strategic-agility`** — *rewiring* is the cell's "rapidly reallocating resources" with a time scale attached: talent, data, capital and decision rights moved in days or weeks rather than quarters.
- **`digital-transforming/redesigning-internal-structures`** — *Imagine* asks for new operating models rather than patched old ones, and *lock-in* codifies what is learned into process, code and policy.
- **`strategic-renewal/organizational-culture`** — *Learn* aims to shift attitudes through widespread experimentation, and *Care* makes employee wellbeing part of the change programme.
