# World Economic Forum

International organisation for public-private cooperation, based in Geneva, best known for its Annual Meeting in **Davos** and for the *Annual Meeting of the New Champions* ("Summer Davos") held in China. In this wiki the WEF appears in **two quite different roles**, and the distinction matters when weighing what it publishes.

**As a publisher.** The WEF runs a podcast portfolio — *Radio Davos*, *Meet the Leader*, *Agenda Dialogues* — whose episodes reach the wiki as video sources. In this role the WEF is a **channel, not an author**: it supplies the interviewer and the framing, while the substantive claims belong to the guest. This is why WEF-published sources carry `author: ["World Economic Forum"]` per the video convention (author = channel) while the guest is named in body prose.

**As a convening body whose events produce other people's data.** Research and surveys are frequently *released at* Davos or presented at WEF meetings and then cited second-hand by others. The wiki holds several such traces, and in none of them is the WEF the researcher.

## Appears in this wiki via

- [[2026-09-10-sundararajan-wef-radio-davos-thrive-in-age-of-ai]] — *Radio Davos*, hosted by **Robin Pomeroy**, **co-produced with China's CGTN** and co-hosted by **Xin Guan**, CGTN's anchor and chief business news editor (10 Sep 2026; ~32:59, ASR captions, 17 chapters). Guest **Arun Sundararajan** of NYU Stern on non-employment work arrangements, the entry-level slowdown, one-person companies, and — for slightly under half the episode — AI governance and sovereignty. **The co-production is the thing to hold in view**: CGTN is a Chinese state broadcaster, the co-host supplies most of the China material, and the episode contains the corpus's most favourable characterisation of Chinese algorithmic governance and industrial policy. See that source's quality note.
- [[2026-07-22-brown-wef-meet-the-leader-entry-level-jobs-in-an-ai-era]] — *Meet the Leader*, hosted by **Linda Lacina**, third instalment of her *AI and Jobs* series (22 Jul 2026). Guest **Peter Brown**, global workforce leader at [[PwC]], interviewed by WEF's Kateryna Gordiychuk at Summer Davos in Tianjin, built around a **WEF–PwC report on AI and entry-level skills**. Here the WEF is a **co-author of the underlying research**, not merely the channel — the one case in the wiki where that is true.

## Cited but not authored

- [[2026-08-01-bbc-ai-decoded-why-isnt-ai-working-for-your-company]] — the panel's headline ROI statistics come from a [[PwC]] survey of ~4,500 CEOs across 95 countries **released at Davos**: 56% yet to see a return on generative-AI investment, only one in eight claiming an actual cost saving or revenue gain. WEF is the venue, PwC the researcher.
- [[2026-05-28-giles-wp-intelligence-new-human-machine-workforce-agentic-ai-jobs]] — reports on a **WEF San Francisco meeting** of manufacturing and supply-chain executives, used as the anchor for the *planners → agent orchestrators* role-shift claim. WEF is the convener; the observation is the reporter's.

## What the wiki has and has not read

**Two WEF-published podcast episodes, both interviews. No WEF report has been ingested directly.** This is a real gap: the WEF's *Future of Jobs Report* is one of the most-cited artefacts in the AI-and-employment literature the wiki tracks across [[ai-employment-effects]] and [[durable-skills]], and the corpus currently reaches it only through other people's summaries — the same second-hand problem flagged on [[PwC]], where three PwC datasets arrive through interviews and none through the published report.

## Open questions

- The **WEF–PwC entry-level skills report** behind the Brown interview has not been ingested. Its sampling frame and the operational definition of *"seniorized skills"* are both load-bearing for how the 7× figure reads, and neither is checkable from the interview.
- The *AI and Jobs* series has three instalments; the wiki holds one. The other two may carry material relevant to [[ai-employment-effects]].
- WEF podcast episodes are **routinely co-produced** with other broadcasters. The CGTN case shows the co-producer can materially shape the content, so the co-production credit should be checked on each future WEF ingest rather than assumed absent.
