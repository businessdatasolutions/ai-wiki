# AI100 (One Hundred Year Study on Artificial Intelligence)

A **longitudinal study of AI** launched in autumn 2014 with an endowment from Eric and Mary Horvitz, and administered out of [[Stanford HAI]]. A Standing Committee, chaired in 2026 by Sheila McIlraith (Toronto), forms a Study Panel every five years. Each panel answers the same twelve "standing questions" anew. The aim is a record over a hundred years or more: *"to distinguish the lasting shifts from passing trends"* and to serve as *"a time capsule"* for future readers. It is modelled on the 2008–2009 "AAAI Asilomar Study" convened by Eric Horvitz.

Reports so far: 2016 (chaired by Peter Stone), 2021, and [[2026-11-10-ai100-ai-goes-mainstream-2026|2026, *AI Goes Mainstream*]], chaired by [[Michael Wooldridge]]. Russ Altman, the faculty director, calls 2026 *"the third or fourth report depending on how you count"*. The 2026 panel of twelve included [[Raymond Perrault]] and [[Toby Walsh]], who also author the [[AI Index]]. The Index was conceived within AI100 in 2017 and reports annually, while AI100 judges five-year periods. The 2026 report closes by auditing its 2021 predecessor claim by claim, a practice the series' design makes possible.
