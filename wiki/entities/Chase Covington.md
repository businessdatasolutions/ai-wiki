# Chase Covington

**Partner at [[McKinsey & Company]]** in New York and a leader in its transformation and strategy and corporate finance practices. He works on large-scale transformations, independent diligence, corporate strategy and financial transactions, and describes himself as a former investment analyst. He co-wrote the McKinsey article *The agentic transformation office: Redefining the economics of change* (not ingested). Promoted to an entity page on 16 November 2026 at his second appearance; both are *Inside the Strategy Room* episodes hosted by [[Sean Brown]], eight days apart.

## Role in the wiki

- **[[2026-11-08-covington-slaney-mckinsey-strategy-room-agentic-transformation|The agentic transformation office (8 Nov 2026)]]**, with Tomer Slaney: agents inside the office that runs a change programme. He names **cognitive surrender** (*"you just let the AI chatbot do your job"*) and sets review rules against it, among them *"if you put something on a page, it's yours."* He says the junior jobs changed rather than went away, and that most transformation-office work *"can be done by open source models."*
- **[[2026-11-16-pralong-mckinsey-strategy-room-gen-ai-outside-in-diligence|Gen AI for outside-in diligence (16 Nov 2026)]]**, with David Pralong, William Bundy and Laura Borton. He gives the episode's adoption figures (88% have launched an AI or digital transformation effort, under 10% operate at scale, 6% of 40 surveyed general partners call the results transformational) and the ratio of *"$5 for adoption and workforce readiness"* per $1 of technology. He splits the staffing effect by organisation type: pyramid compression in IT services, little change in lean private-equity teams.

## Recurring positions

- Adoption problems are cultural and organisational more than technical: *"more so than better models or better LLMs."*
- Model choice matters less than fitting the workflow to its context.
- The value of AI in a small expert team is better decisions and better use of time, not fewer people.

## Open questions

- Both appearances are on McKinsey's own podcast, describing McKinsey services. Neither of the McKinsey articles the two episodes accompany is in the wiki, and the surveys he cites are unnamed.
