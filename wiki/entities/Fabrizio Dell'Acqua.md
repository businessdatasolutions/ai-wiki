# Fabrizio Dell'Acqua

Researcher at the Digital Data Design Institute / HBS AI Institute at Harvard Business School, and **the corpus's most-cited author of field-experimental evidence on generative AI and knowledge work**.

Two papers, both in *Organization Science*, both preregistered field experiments with large professional samples, and together the wiki's strongest empirical line on what AI does to people doing real work:

- [[2026-04-28-dellacqua-jagged-technological-frontier|*Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier*]] — the BCG study that named the [[jagged-frontier]] and measured **individuals** against an uneven capability boundary.
- [[2026-06-12-dellacqua-cybernetic-teammate-field-experiment-genai-teamwork|*The Cybernetic Teammate*]] — 791 professionals at Procter & Gamble, measuring **teams**: AI-equipped individuals matched two-person teams, dissolved functional silos, and improved self-reported affect, while human judgment held its value in evaluative selection.

Read as a pair they move the same question up a level — from *how well does a person do with AI* to *can AI stand in for a colleague* — and the second paper leaves the first's construct explicitly open, naming whether humans' mental models *"track the technology's jagged capabilities"* as a critical unresolved frontier.

Frequent co-authors in this corpus: [[Ethan Mollick]] and [[Karim Lakhani]], both on the *Cybernetic Teammate* author list.
