# Bloomberg Podcasts

The podcast arm of **Bloomberg**'s business and financial news operation, publishing under **Bloomberg Audio Studios**. It runs many strands; two are represented in this wiki.

- ***Trumponomics*** — a weekly economics show hosted by **Stephanie Flanders**, Bloomberg's editorial head of government and economics, examining the economic policies of the second Trump administration and their global consequences.
- ***Bloomberg Leaders*** — long-form interviews with chief executives about how they run their companies.

Promoted to a wiki entity page on 12 August 2026 under the [second-source promotion rule](../../CLAUDE.md#author-entity-promotion), on the addition of the Frey interview alongside the earlier Erginbiç episode.

## Role in the wiki

Bloomberg Podcasts is the wiki's **financial-press vantage** on the AI economy — distinct from the practitioner and consulting sources that dominate the corpus, and distinct in turn from [[BBC]]'s public-service broadcast framing. Where a consulting source describes what firms should do and a vendor source describes what its customers are doing, this venue's contribution is the macro-economic and markets frame: whether the aggregate numbers move at all, and what that implies for policy.

Its most substantive contribution to date is [[2026-08-05-frey-bloomberg-trumponomics-why-ai-isnt-boosting-productivity|the Frey interview]], which supplies the wiki's clearest macro-level mechanism for why task-level AI gains have not aggregated — the **verification tax** ("it's the time saving minus the time for verification") — plus the argument that the computer revolution was the more transformative technology and still yielded only a decade-long, largely American productivity upsurge. See [[concepts/micro-productivity-trap|micro-productivity-trap]] and [[concepts/enterprise-ai-adoption|enterprise-ai-adoption]].

## Source-quality posture

Bloomberg's interview formats are host-led and generally give the guest room to make their case; the Frey episode is a book-promotion interview in which the host does put the strongest counter-argument. Treat guests' empirical claims as reported rather than verified — Bloomberg's editorial standards apply to the framing, not to a guest's uncited research findings.
