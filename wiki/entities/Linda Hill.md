# Linda Hill

Wallace Brett Donham Professor of Business Administration at **Harvard Business School** and faculty chair of its **Leadership Initiative**; principal of the leadership-advisory consultancy **Paradox Strategies**. Her research programme is leadership and innovation — how organisations produce collective creative output, and what leaders do to make that possible. Books include *Becoming a Manager*, *Collective Genius* and *Genius at Scale*.

Her work reaches this wiki through four constructs, all of which arrive in the corpus attached to her name:

- **Creative abrasion** — the organisational capacity to surface and productively use disagreement. Her prescription is directional and blunt: *"you need to amplify difference. But many CEOs minimize difference."*
- **Contextual intelligence** — reading what a person's context makes salient before attributing their behaviour to character, and asking which constraint, if relieved, would change it.
- **Wayfinding vs. pathfinding** — her named distinction for leading when no destination can be posited. *"The wayfinding is we don't know the destination, nonetheless how we're going to get there."* She applies it directly to AI, arguing that leaders' inability to state an AI vision is structurally correct for breakthrough innovation rather than a failure of strategy.
- **Empowerment as an overrated practice** — because *"empowerment simply means that you're sharing with people the potential to have influence. So whether or not they get to exercise that potential is your call."*
