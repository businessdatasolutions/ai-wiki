# Holly Price

Associate partner in **[[McKinsey & Company]]**'s Houston office, working on people and organization. She is coauthor of McKinsey's 2026 AI readiness survey and its public voice on McKinsey Talks Talent.

Promoted to an entity page on 27 November 2026. That day the article she coauthored was ingested, and she had been listed as Dangling on the podcast page. She is an `author:` on one page and a guest on the other. As with [[George Westerman]], the page exists because both sources give her views at length; the [§Author-entity promotion](../../CLAUDE.md#author-entity-promotion) rule alone would not require it.

## What she contributes

- **[[2026-07-08-de-smet-mckinsey-three-horizons-of-ai-transformation|From adoption to impact (McKinsey Quarterly, 8 Jul 2026)]]:** one of seven authors of a survey of 750 employees. It defines three horizons (enablement, automation, reinvention) and the readiness factors linked to reported value in each.
- **[[2026-11-24-price-mckinsey-talks-talent-employees-more-ready-for-ai|McKinsey Talks Talent (24 Nov 2026)]]:** her account of the same survey, with the episode's pull-quote about CEOs who use AI *"to plan a vacation."* She argues leaders should model AI use and listen to employees who say it is not working.

## Recurring positions

- **Trust before reassurance.** Telling employees nothing will change lowers anxiety without earning trust (both sources).
- **Value needs redesign, not access.** Tools for individuals rarely produce enterprise value unless workflows change (both sources).

The podcast presents the horizon split as where organizations are; the article's own method note says the sample was tilted toward advanced horizons. See the [[2026-07-08-de-smet-mckinsey-three-horizons-of-ai-transformation|article page]].
