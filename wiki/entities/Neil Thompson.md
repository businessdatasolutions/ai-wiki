# Neil Thompson

**Director of the MIT FutureTech research project**, cross-appointed at MIT's Computer Science and AI Laboratory (CSAIL) and MIT's Initiative on the Digital Economy. FutureTech studies the economic and technical foundations of progress in computing. Previously Assistant Professor of Innovation and Strategy at MIT Sloan (co-directed the Experimental Innovation Lab / X-Lab) and Visiting Professor at Harvard's Laboratory for Innovation Science. PhD in Business and Public Policy (Berkeley, with Masters in Computer Science and Statistics); Masters in Economics (LSE); undergraduate degrees in Physics and International Development. Prior to academia: Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, Bain & Company, the United Nations, the World Bank, the Canadian Parliament.

Surfaces in this wiki as co-author (with **David Autor**) of the **expertise framework** — a theoretical model of occupational task bundling and expertise hierarchy explaining why automation simultaneously raises wages in some occupations and lowers them in others, depending on whether the automated tasks were relatively expert or inexpert within the occupation's task bundle.
