# Thread: Dynamic capabilities and the Frontier Firm

## The question

The wiki holds [[2026-04-28-warner-wager-dynamic-capabilities-digital-transformation|Warner & Wäger's process model]] as its tagging vocabulary, and, since 2026-11-22, the two fullest statements of the Frontier Firm: [[2026-09-16-microsoft-becoming-a-frontier-firm-second-edition|Microsoft's Frontier Playbook]] and the [[2026-11-19-george-hrncirik-ignite-becoming-a-frontier-firm|Ignite 2026 session]] presenting it.

**The question this thread tracks:** *How does the Frontier Firm relate to Warner & Wäger's dynamic-capabilities process model: where do they coincide, where do they diverge, and what does reading one through the other add?*

It also takes up, for one framework, an open question in [[syntheses/organizational-frameworks-for-ai-adoption|organizational-frameworks-for-ai-adoption]]: a cross-walk between the practitioner frameworks and academic strategy theory.

## Why this thread opened and closed in the same session

The user asked for the synthesis directly, the day the Frontier Firm cluster reached nine sources. The material was gathered with the iterative wiki query (research log: `inspiration/2026-11-22-dynamic-capabilities-frontier-firm-research-log.md`).

## Closed

Filed [[syntheses/dynamic-capabilities-and-the-frontier-firm|the synthesis]] on 2026-11-22. Headline: the Frontier Firm reads as an AI-era operationalisation of W&W's transforming cluster. Its practices are mostly *lock-in*, the codifying of what a team learns. It leaves strategic sensing, portfolio balancing, ecosystems and business-model renewal almost empty. It also moves inimitability from tacit knowledge to codified knowledge protected by architecture, a claim no source tests.
