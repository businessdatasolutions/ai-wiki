How an LLM Wiki works

Plain RAG rediscovers knowledge from raw chunks on every question. An LLM Wiki has the model compile each source into persistent, interlinked markdown pages and keep them current. Retrieval doesn't go away: questions search the wiki and the raw sources, and valuable answers go back into the wiki after review.

The three operations

  1. Ingest. You add a source. In stage 1 (analysis) the LLM extracts entities and concepts, links them to the existing wiki and detects contradictions. In stage 2 (generation) it writes the source summary, updates or creates entity and concept pages, updates index.md and log.md, and files review items. One source can touch 10 to 15 pages.
  2. Query. The LLM searches the wiki, starting from the index, and the raw sources. It packs the selected pages, log history and raw passages into the context window and answers with citations. A valuable answer is saved back as a page, after review.
  3. Lint. Now and then the LLM health-checks the wiki: orphan pages, stale claims, missing cross-references, concepts without a page.

The three layers

RAW SOURCES

Your curated documents. Immutable: the LLM reads them and never edits them.

LLM WIKI

Markdown pages the LLM writes and owns: sources, entities, concepts and syntheses, linked into a graph, plus an index, a log and a review queue for contradictions and answers that need a human decision.

SCHEMA

A rulebook such as CLAUDE.md that tells the LLM how to ingest, answer and maintain the wiki.

Where to start

SituationStart with
Mostly one-off FAQ lookupClassic RAG
Repeated analysis over the same sourcesAdd an LLM Wiki layer
Relationships and contradictions matterWiki plus graph
Identical output is requiredDon't rely on automatic wiki generation alone
Raw details decide the answerUse the wiki as a map, then return to raw sources

The weak points: information loss when sources are summarized, summary drift as pages are rewritten, error cementing when a bad summary becomes context for later questions, and non-determinism. The same source won't always produce the same wiki.
